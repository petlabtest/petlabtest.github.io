Nicola D'Ascenzo

Professor, Department of Biomedical Engineering, School of Life Science and Technology, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, and a researcher at the Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics. In the international scientific and technological exchanges between China and Italy, he was awarded the honorary title of "Ambassador of the Molise Region" by the President of Italy, and is a recipient of the National High-Level Talent Program.

  • Department: Department of Biomedical Engineering, School of Life Science and Technology, Huazhong University of Science and Technology
  • Address: Room 408, Enming Building, Huazhong University of Science and Technology
  • Email: ndasc@hust.edu.cn

Focuses on research of photon response mechanisms and signal processing methods in single-photon detection chips, as well as novel PET system design and imaging methodologies (including helmet PET and Agri-PET).

Dedicated to the development of all-digital silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs) and portable PET imaging equipment. To establish a new paradigm for "real-time processing of single-photon signal spatiotemporal information," developed high-detection-efficiency, low-noise CMOS SiPM sensor technology and novel all-digital Multi-Threshold (MT)-SiPM devices, truly approaching the physical limits of SiPM response speed and information extraction accuracy. To achieve precise diagnosis and treatment of brain diseases, developed a novel helmet-style all-digital PET system featuring a hemispherical configuration adaptable to head shape. Its high sensitivity and count rate capability enable short dynamic frame acquisition, making high-resolution dynamic imaging of freely moving patients possible.

Has successively led or participated as a key member in 5 national major research projects, with 12 patents applied for and granted. Recent publications in IEEE EDL and IEEE TED report research advances in CMOS SiPM technology, developing high-detection-efficiency, low-noise CMOS SiPM sensor technology to address critical challenges in photodetection devices; a publication in Chinese Optics Letters introduced a new paradigm for real-time spatiotemporal information processing of single-photon signals, providing novel methods for all-digital MT-SiPM devices to approach physical detection limits; research in IEEE TRPMS and TNS demonstrated the implementation of the novel helmet-style all-digital PET system, enabling high-resolution dynamic imaging of the human brain during free movement; innovatively proposed plant PET imaging methods, expanding PET applications to new fields. Has cumulatively published over 300 papers in related journals and conferences including Physical Review Letters, IEEE Electron Device Letters, Physical Review D, IEEE Transactions on Radiation and Plasma Medical Sciences, Applied Mathematics Letters, and the IEEE Nuclear Science Symposium and Medical Imaging Conference (NSS/MIC).
