Xinhua News Agency, Wuhan, November 27 (Reporter Tang Weibin and Yu Jian) A team of 13 interdisciplinary experts led by Professor Xie Qingguo from the School of Life Sciences at Huazhong University of Science and Technology has overcome the technical challenges in digitalizing high-end medical equipment PET after eleven years of effort, successfully developing a small-sized digital PET with independent intellectual property rights.

In recent more than one year of animal experiments, it was found that its main performance indicators, such as imaging spatial resolution, have reached 0.87 millimeters, meaning the smallest detectable lesion is 0.66 cubic millimeters, bringing good news to cancer patients.

In May this year, experts organized by the Hubei Provincial Department of Science and Technology on behalf of China's Ministry of Science and Technology concluded that this achievement utilizes self-developed key technologies in PET high-speed scintillation pulse digital acquisition and processing at an international leading level, solving critical issues in PET applications and achieving a technological breakthrough for China in the field of PET.

Positron Emission Tomography (PET) is one of the most advanced medical imaging techniques after ultrasound, CT, and magnetic resonance imaging. It has become one of the best means for clinical diagnosis and guiding cancer treatment. Due to its involvement with nuclear physics, electronics, materials science, mechanics, medicine, and other disciplines, it has high technical barriers; currently, only three multinational companies worldwide can independently develop and produce PET imaging equipment.

Professor Xie Qingguo, who also serves as a researcher at the Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics (WNLFO) in the Department of Biomedical Photonics, introduced that since its inception in the 1970s, PET devices have been constrained by technical bottlenecks such as difficulty in digitalizing ultra-high-speed scintillation pulses, resulting in only analog and hybrid analog-digital machines being available.

In 2003, Professor Xie Qingguo proposed the 'multi-voltage threshold sampling method' and continuously improved it under support from the National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC), Ministry of Science and Technology, and Ministry of Education, ultimately overcoming this world-class challenge in PET digitalization.

Professor Xie Qingguo led his team to complete the entire R&D process through an innovative model integrating original research, industrial operation, and clinical application. After developing the world's first small-sized digital PET machine in November 2010, they are now advancing towards clinical applications and industrial production.

Since November 2011 until now, Professor Xie Qingguo’s team has completed studies on 59 mice including 13 cases of lung cancer, liver cancer, ovarian cancer, etc., 16 cases of Alzheimer's disease mice, and 30 normal mice, comprehensively verifying the excellent performance of small-sized digital PET machines.

Zhang Yongxue, a senior expert in PET clinical applications at Wuhan Union Hospital and director of its PET Center, stated that any improvement in resolution is revolutionary progress in medicine, meaning longer life for patients and better timing and precision for doctors.

Professor Jianmin Gao, a tenured professor at the University of Chicago and a renowned expert in PET imaging, commented, 'Professor Xie Qingguo proposed multi-voltage threshold sampling technology, achieving direct digitalization of ultra-high-speed scintillation pulses with an elegant and concise method, pioneering full-digital PET, which is expected to bring about significant upgrades and leapfrog development for China's advanced medical imaging equipment.'

IEEE TNS reviewers commented in their paper reviews that 'this signal processing method is highly innovative in the field of PET and has great engineering application potential,' and 'digital PET demonstrates superior performance on a system level.' Experts from the National Natural Science Foundation of China evaluated that 'digital PET has prominent competitive advantages in hardware, software, and signal processing methods, with significant research and medical application value, contributing to progress in these fields.'

Currently, there are over 5000 commercially available PET machines worldwide. Due to high purchase and maintenance costs, fewer than 200 units are used clinically in China, leaving a huge market potential. It is reported that Professor Xie Qingguo's team has complete independent intellectual property rights for digital PET with 17 authorized patents. They plan to develop a full-body human prototype machine for clinical trials to quickly bring digital PET into application and benefit humanity.
