(Hubei Daily, June 10, 2019, Page 01)

Hubei Daily (Reporters Fang Lin, Dai Jinsong, Communicator Yang Ya) On June 6th, it was reported by Huazhong University of Science and Technology that Professor Xie Qingguo led the development of domestically produced full digital PET. After 19 years of hard work, this product finally obtained market access and sales qualifications. This means that China's full digital PET with independent intellectual property rights can now enter mass production and will break the long-term monopoly of imported instruments in the domestic PET market.

PET, or Positron Emission Tomography, is a crucial device for precision medicine, mainly used for pathological diagnosis of tumors, cardiovascular diseases, etc., alongside CT and MRI as the three major components of medical imaging. For a long time, due to extremely high technical barriers, PET research has been monopolized by Western multinational giants, becoming one of the bottlenecks in China's healthcare sector. 'Fully following the technological route of multinational corporations can significantly reduce development risks but will always result in being a follower. Over 19 years, we have finally charted an entirely new technological path, fundamentally avoiding intellectual property risks.' Professor Xie Qingguo believes that full digital PET, from innovative scientific principles at its source to the development of clinical equipment with globally leading performance, fully meets all requirements for innovative medical devices.

In 2015, Ezhou City Government introduced full digital PET as a major technological innovation project and established an industrial entity. They successfully developed the world's first large-scale clinical full digital PET, whose core indicators are comprehensively superior to international counterparts.

In July 2018, clinical full digital PET completed clinical trials at Sun Yat-sen University Affiliated First Hospital and Affiliated Cancer Hospital in Guangzhou, verifying its safety and effectiveness for imaging diagnosis. Mr. Luo, a 38-year-old nasopharyngeal cancer patient, participated in the clinical trial. Using digital PET for prognosis evaluation, it was discovered that cancer cells had metastasized to his scapula, ribs, and femur. This early detection and precise localization enabled timely treatment for Mr. Luo, who is now basically recovered.

Zhang Bo, the industrialization manager of this instrument, stated that full digital PET solved the 'ultra-high-speed flash signal digitization' problem that has plagued traditional PET development for over 40 years, allowing earlier and more sensitive detection of various lesions including tumors. It can diagnose cancer, Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's syndrome, etc., and after achieving widespread development, it will significantly lower the threshold for public access to its services, with broad application prospects.

(Hubei Daily, June 10, 2019, Page 08)

"I am proud that we have contributed full digital PET to the world"

Hubei Daily Full Media Reporters Fang Lin, Dai Jinsong Communicator Yang Ya

"Don’t Panic" The prominent sign on Professor Xie Qingguo's office door comes from the science fiction classic 'The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy,' reminding space travelers entering unknown territories — don't panic. 'In the path of scientific research, one must not be panicked either.' For this reason, Professor Xie and his team have been trekking for 19 years until June 4th, 2019 afternoon when the National Medical Products Administration announced in a notice on the issuance of production permits: “Positron Emission Tomography and X-ray Computerized Tomography” (Application No.: CQZ1800580) has been approved. The full digital PET he invented officially entered mass production.

The team was established in 2001;

In 2004, the MVT full digital PET method was proposed;

In 2007, the world's first flat-panel PET was realized;

On December 2010, the first digital PET was unveiled;

In August 2015, the world's first clinical whole-body full digital PET system was successfully developed;

In November 2017, the world's first proton PET prototype machine was successfully developed;

In August 2018, the clinical whole-body full digital PET/CT completed its clinical trial;

In September 2018, the world's first brain-specific full digital PET was successfully developed.

At every step, Xie Qingguo has moved steadily. 'The digital PET finally obtained its first medical device registration certificate; I am grateful for the leadership’s vision and courage, teachers’ attention and care, team’s sentiment and perseverance, colleagues’ encouragement and tolerance. Previously, we described advancements in PET technology by mentioning 32 slices, 64 slices, or even 128 slices, with more slices indicating higher performance. Now, the term 'full digital PET' itself is a significant contribution of ours. I am very proud.'

This is a brand new thing; all new things go through a long process to be recognized by the public. The relationship between full digital PET and traditional PET is akin to that between digital cameras and film cameras, representing a revolutionary technological innovation and a qualitative leap. Xie Qingguo understands this well. For domestic high-end medical devices to lead in technology, product quality, and market share, it does not happen overnight; it requires substantial practical applications to continuously improve the products and innovate technologies.

Academician Ni Jiazuan of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, who has collaborated with Xie Qingguo's team on instrument application projects, believes that full digital PET, from key materials to core components and system integration, is all independently developed in China. This represents a hallmark achievement of independent innovation. 'For a long time, the core technology of PET has been controlled by international giants. Now that domestic full digital PET has obtained market access, the most critical aspect is to quickly achieve mass production and scale up operations, continuously refining the new technological route through large-scale applications so that domestically developed high-end medical devices can benefit the public sooner.'

2015 was a memorable year for Xie Qingguo's team. That year, 13 people who had never seen a PET before built a clinical full digital PET in just eight and a half months. 'We have nurtured a promising child with our best years of life over the past two decades. We hope that more people will join us to nurture and cultivate him so he can grow up healthy.'
