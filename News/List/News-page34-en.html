Xie Qingguo: Contributing Fully Digital PET to the World

After 19 years, Xie Qingguo and his team have pioneered a new digital PET technology route, driving China's original high-end medical devices from scientific principles towards clinical and industrial applications.
December 9, 2020
Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics

"Don’t Panic" The prominent sign on Professor Xie Qingguo’s office door, taken from the science fiction classic “The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy,” reminds interstellar travelers entering unknown space—don't panic. "In the path of scientific research, one mustn't panic either." For this reason, Xie Qingguo and his team have been trekking on this road for 19 years until June 4th, 2019 afternoon when the National Medical Products Administration announced the “Approval Notice for Production Permit”: The “Positron Emission Tomography and X-ray Computerized Tomography Imaging System” (Acceptance Number: CQZ1800580) has passed approval. His invention of domestically produced fully digital PET officially entered mass production.

  1. 2001, the team was established;
  2. 2004, proposed MVT fully digital PET method;
  3. 2007, achieved the world's first flat-panel PET;
  4. December 2013, the first digital PET was unveiled;
  5. August 2015, successfully developed the world’s first clinical whole-body fully digital PET system;
  6. November 2017, the world's first proton PET prototype machine was successfully developed;
  7. August 2018, the clinical whole-body fully digital PET/CT completed clinical trials;
  8. September 2018, the world's first brain-specific fully digital PET was successfully developed.

At every step, Xie Qingguo has walked with strength and determination. 'Digital PET has finally obtained its first medical device registration certificate; I am grateful for the leadership's vision and courage, my mentors' attention and care, the team's passion and perseverance, and colleagues' encouragement and tolerance. In the past, we described advancements in PET technology by mentioning 32 slices, 64 slices, or even 128 slices, with more slices indicating higher sophistication. Now, the term 'all-digital PET' itself is a significant contribution of ours. I am very proud.

This is a brand new thing; all new things go through a long process of being recognized by the public. The relationship between fully digital PET and traditional PET is akin to that between digital cameras and film cameras, representing a revolutionary technological innovation, a qualitative leap forward. Xie Qingguo is well aware of this point. For domestically produced high-end medical equipment, moving from technical leadership to product leadership and then to market leadership does not happen overnight; it requires substantial, down-to-earth applications, continuously improving the products and innovating technologies through these applications.

Academician Jiazuan Ni, who has previously collaborated with Professor Qingguo Xie's team on instrument applications at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, believes that fully digital PET, from key materials and core components to system integration, is entirely an independently developed achievement. 'For a long time, the core technology of PET has been in the hands of international giants. Now that domestically produced full-digital PET has obtained market access, the most critical thing is to quickly achieve mass production and scale-up development, continuously improving this new technological route through large-scale applications so that high-end medical devices can benefit the people as soon as possible.'

"1. Persistently Achieving International Originality"

PET is an abbreviation for Positron Emission Tomography, a highly sensitive nuclear medicine molecular imaging technique. It has significant application value in the basic research, early diagnosis, and efficacy evaluation of major diseases such as malignant tumors, cardiovascular diseases, and neurological disorders.

“In the past, PET products created by GPS (GE, Philips, Siemens) have made outstanding contributions to precise diagnosis and treatment of major diseases, and also formed a mature technical system. Fully following the technological route of GPS can significantly reduce the risk of product development, but one will always remain a follower.” Professor Qingguo Xie, the inventor of fully digital PET and professor at Huazhong University of Science and Technology, said.

In 2001, Xie Qingguo established his laboratory. In 2004, he first proposed the MVT full digital sampling method; in 2009, he developed a fully digital and modular PET detector. In 2010, the world's first digital PET scientific instrument was unveiled; in 2015, Ezhou Municipal Committee and Municipal Government listed Digital PET as 'Project No. 1 for Scientific and Technological Transformation', and in August of that year, the world's first clinical full digital PET device was launched. In 2018, full digital PET/CT completed its clinical trials.

Zhang Bo is a PhD student in the School of Life Sciences at Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Class of 2018, and also one of the industrialization leaders for the Digital PET project. Nineteen years ago, young scholar Xie Qingguo chose PET as his research direction. In 2004, Xie Qingguo invented digital PET technology; in the same year, Zhang Bo came to Huazhong University to study for his undergraduate degree and joined Professor Xie's research team in 2005. From undergraduate to master’s studies, over a period of seven years, Zhang Bo witnessed the arduous journey of Digital PET transforming from theoretical invention into technology and products.

When Professor Xie first proposed the concept of digital PET, it was not well received by the industry. However, he persisted in cultivating this uncharted territory of digital PET. Gradually, international medical device giants began to recognize the value and potential of digital PET; Siemens started proposing digital methods in 2009, followed by Philips introducing its own digital technology in 2010. Nowadays, among PET manufacturers, there is a sudden surge of 'full digital' solutions. This has made me realize that on the path towards pursuing truth, as long as one sees clearly and firmly pursues their goal without giving up, they will surely see the light after the clouds have dispersed! Zhang Bo said.

In 2014, Xie Qingguo, who was preparing for the clinical industrialization of PET, approached Zhang Bo, who had already graduated with a master's degree: 'Do you want to do a job that you can see yourself doing until retirement, or are you willing to embrace a lifelong career filled with challenges and opportunities?' For Zhang Bo, Digital PET is a hard technology that he has been relentlessly pursuing for over a decade, possessing immense technical potential and application value. 'What I was missing all along is the sense of belonging to dedicate my life to a cause; Digital PET is my lifelong career!' It was with this mindset that Zhang Bo joined without hesitation.

'Starting a business is always the hardest path!' Looking back on the past fourteen years of struggling alongside his mentor, Zhang Bo is filled with countless emotions. At first, there was no space available, so everyone had to set up in an abandoned laboratory corner at school. In a forty-square-meter venue, ten or more people squeezed together; when assembling prototype machines, every table had to be moved back and forth repeatedly to make room; during the most difficult times, they couldn't even pay salaries, but everyone gritted their teeth and managed to persevere through tight finances.

“Now, digital PET has finally obtained its first medical device registration certificate, which means that we have truly achieved an international original innovation. This new technological route has been verified to be viable and correct!” said Xie Qingguo. “What makes us most proud is that we defined ‘digital PET’. As a gold standard for measuring the advanced nature of PET products, digital PET has, in a sense, become a benchmark and a definition.”

“This is also due to the Ministry of Science and Technology, the National Natural Science Foundation of China, and local science and technology departments recognizing the enormous potential of digital PET, boldly acting as ‘angel investors’, supporting the team all along.” Xie Qingguo stated that currently, the team has cumulatively received over 200 million yuan in various project funds, with more than 500 patent applications made, of which over 200 have been authorized.

"Part II: Breaking Through Technical Routes in 'Hell Mode'"

With limited resources, the current forensic product can be said to have adopted a system design with the highest technical challenge difficulty, akin to choosing 'Hell Mode' in a game. By overcoming the technology route in the most difficult mode, subsequent work becomes effortless! This fully demonstrates the immense potential of digital PET, this brand-new technology.

In fact, the advantages brought by the digitalization of PET are not only reflected in its ability to detect smaller tumor lesions, enabling early diagnosis and treatment. Its highly modular design also offers rapid research and development as well as quick assembly advantages. According to reports, it took only nine months from design to completion for the first clinical full-digital PET prototype machine in 2015, with significantly fewer personnel and resources invested compared to others.

Another example is the first brain-specific full-digital PET installed at Sun Yat-sen University's First Affiliated Hospital. This equipment was completed in just three months and has since conducted multiple brain imaging trials, soon to be widely used in diagnosing and researching major brain diseases such as Alzheimer's disease and Parkinson's disease. In August 2019, a 30-year-old woman was diagnosed with Parkinson's disease by this brain-specific digital PET, breaking into tears upon receiving the report. Parkinson's disease is a common neurodegenerative disorder that typically affects people around age 60, predominantly older adults; however, in recent years, there has been an increasing trend of younger patients.

“Although Parkinson's disease cannot be cured, it is fortunate that this young patient was diagnosed early with PET. As long as rehabilitation treatment and scientific medication are initiated at the initial stage of the illness, the progression of the disease can be significantly delayed, thereby maintaining longer working hours and self-care capabilities. With proper management, patients with Parkinson's disease can have a lifespan comparable to healthy individuals,” said Professor Zhang Xiangsong, head of the Nuclear Medicine Department at Sun Yat-sen University’s First Affiliated Hospital.

According to data from national cancer registration points released in February 2017: China's five-year survival rate for cancer patients is only 30.9%, less than half that of the United States. One of the key reasons for low survival rates among Chinese cancer patients lies in delayed detection and treatment of cancers. It is known that currently, the number of PETs per million people in the US and Japan exceeds four, whereas China has fewer than 0.3 units per million people. Once domestically produced clinical full-digital PET achieves mass production, it can significantly lower the threshold for public access to PET examination services.

Three: Forming an Innovation Loop, Hard to be Held Back

The digital PET developed by Professor Xie Qingguo's team covers the entire innovation chain from principle, system to application. The industrial chain connects key materials such as scintillation crystals, core devices like SiPMs (Silicon Photomultipliers), critical components of digital PET detectors, animal full-digital PET scientific instruments, clinical full-digital PET medical equipment, and imaging service applications, forming an innovation loop that is technologically hard for anyone to hold back.

Weak light detection chips are core components of PET, long monopolized by the West. To avoid being constrained in the industrial chain in the future, Professor Xie Qingguo's team started laying out research and development of new optoelectronic conversion devices from 2006 and began to introduce international talents in related fields.

“The emergence of this leading digital PET technology has brought about a chain reaction of innovation across the entire PET industry, attracting top-notch talent at every link.” Professor Xie Qingguo introduced that besides himself, other professors on his team are also new or old elites in the field: Russian professor Valeri Saveliev from the Russian Engineering Physics Institute is one of the inventors of silicon photomultipliers and a leading expert in optoelectronic devices; Italian professor Nicola D'Ascenzo from Hamburg University specializes in high-energy particle physics and detection technology; Professor Xiao Peng from National University of Singapore focuses on novel system design and image reconstruction for digital PET.

In 2017, the team successfully developed fully digital silicon photomultipliers (SiPM) using standard CMOS process technology, achieving an internationally advanced level. This successful development is significant for independent innovation in China's weak-light optoelectronic detection technology and for strengthening the country's core industrial competitiveness.

“We are doing world-class research, of course we need to gather top talents globally.” When talking about the two foreign professors, Professor Xie Qingguo introduced that they had worked here for quite some time before joining full-time. “In late 2014, they decided to work full-time in China. Nicola even moved his entire family to Wuhan, and his wife also joined Huazhong University of Science and Technology; their two daughters have become 'new Chinese citizens'."

“Foreign experts are very pure, compared with the standards abroad, their salaries here are not high,” said Professor Xie Qingguo. “They all value this research platform at the world's forefront in digital PET technology. Through industrialization, these scientists' technologies can be widely applied and thus have a continuous life span; that is what they value most.”

Professor Xiao Peng added that overall, China’s environment has changed. In the past, talents would go abroad for development, but now this situation is reversing. “Talent outflow has turned into talent inflow,” he said.

The reversal is not just in the flow of talent.

With the formation of a research high ground centered around digital PET, the team continuously outputs technology and products. “China is reversing from being an importer to an exporter of technology—this is also part of 'Chinese confidence,'” Professor Xiao Peng said.

In July 2017, the team established the Digital PET Molise Research Branch (PETLab@NEUROMED) in Italy, which is the first overseas research branch set up by China within Italian territory. Both parties will collaborate on brain science research here. In January 2018, this center officially began to recruit professors and students globally.

The team also maintains long-term cooperation with numerous prestigious universities, research institutions, and teaching-research hospitals worldwide, including the University of Chicago, Enrico Fermi Institute, UC Berkeley, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, Fermilab (Fermi National Accelerator Center), Moscow Engineering Physics Institute SiPM Lab, CERN (European Organization for Nuclear Research), Finland’s national PET center, among others.

Xie Qingguo stated that prestigious universities around the world are now engaged in PET research, as are many Chinese universities. “One point we take pride in is that our university is the only one among those researching PET without a nuclear physics or high-energy physics department. Other universities—for example, the University of Chicago—are birthplaces of nuclear physics, associated with early figures such as Fermi and the first reactors. We, however, drew on our strengths in engineering and medicine, entered through digital technology, and succeeded with an unconventional approach.”

After 19 years of arduous efforts, Xie Qingguo and his team have pioneered a new path for digital PET technology, fundamentally avoiding the risk of intellectual property rights issues. Today, digital PET has progressed from scientific principle innovation to clinical whole-machine equipment, and finally obtained market entry qualifications, indicating that we not only completed the transition from prototype to product (0 to 1), but also initiated the process from product to commodity (1 to many)!

Xie Qingguo stated that digital PET was born in China and has established a clear technological advantage. The research platform and clinical platform using digital PET are gathering an increasing number of world-class scientists and clinical experts teams. Such progress is the result of the country's steady advancement of its innovation-driven strategy. Although new technologies and products go through a long process from niche recognition to widespread acceptance, especially in high-end medical equipment fields, more decision-makers and research entities, as well as market players, are willing to try Chinese innovations and support them. As China’s technological strength grows, the previous situation of talent outflow and technology importation is reversing. Digital PET serves as a typical example.
