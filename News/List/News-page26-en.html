China's original all-digital PET enters clinical stable operation

Liu Zhiwei, correspondent of Science and Technology Daily, Yang Ya, learned from Huazhong University of Science and Technology on April 8 that the world's first clinical all-digital PET invented by the university's Xie Qingguo team was put into trial operation in Ezhou Central Hospital in Hubei Province last June...
2021-06-23
Science and Technology Daily

Reporter Liu Zhiwei and correspondent Yang Ya from Science and Technology Daily

On April 8, it was reported by Huazhong University of Science and Technology that the world's first clinical full-digital PET invented by Professor Xie Qingguo’s team has been in trial operation at Ezhou Central Hospital in Hubei Province since last June. This 'sharp-eyed' 'cancer early warning machine' has detected cancer lesions for hundreds of patients ahead of time, completing a challenging innovation process from research and development to clinical application, achieving a leapfrogging development from zero to one for domestically innovated medical instruments.

“Because it is digitalized and modularized, maintenance is particularly easy. If there is an issue with any part, only the faulty module needs to be replaced; unlike traditional PETs where fixing one small problem often requires extensive effort and time.” Dr. Jiao Cisi, head of nuclear medicine at Ezhou Central Hospital, said that compared to traditional PETs, the most distinctive feature of digital PET devices is their ease of use.

　　

Professor Xie Qingguo, the inventor of full-digital PET, is inspecting the equipment

For patients, the most direct experience is a quick and accurate examination. If the examinee accidentally moves on the examination bed causing image artifacts or if an unexpected interruption occurs during the check-up, digital PET does not require a re-scan but can correct it through image processing software. It achieves both speed and accuracy in diagnosis while reducing radiation dose.

PET stands for Positron Emission Tomography, a highly sensitive nuclear medicine molecular imaging technology. In basic research, early diagnosis, and treatment evaluation of major diseases such as malignant tumors, cardiovascular disease, and neurological disorders, PET has significant application value.

Due to the extremely high threshold for R&D, only a few Western countries previously mastered PET development technology. Professor Xie Qingguo’s team solved the international challenge of digitalizing the signal source in PET through their MVT method, achieving originality and leadership in global digital PET research fields. After entering clinical practice, they proved the feasibility of this new technological route with concrete clinical cases, demonstrating the enormous potential of this disruptive innovation.

Professor Xie Qingguo introduced that the first PET was born in a small town in America, while the world's first original full-digital PET originated from Ezhou City in Hubei Province, China. This fully demonstrates that whether a major scientific and technological achievement can be successfully transformed depends not only on the strength of the R&D team but also on how bold the local government is to promote it and support it with all efforts.

“Currently, we have completed the research and application breakthrough from zero to one in Ezhou. Next, digital PET needs to quickly achieve large-scale and high-quality applications, entering a fast lane for development from one to many.” Professor Xie Qingguo expressed his expectations.
