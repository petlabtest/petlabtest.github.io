Digital PET invented in mainland China is used for proton beam monitoring for the first time in Taiwan

Recently, for the first time, mainland scientists used all-digital flat PET for online monitoring of proton beams at Changgeng Memorial Hospital in Taiwan, and achieved good results...
2017-06-28
Digital PET Laboratory

Recently, mainland scientists conducted the first experiment at Chang Gung Memorial Hospital in Taiwan using all-digital flat-panel PET for online monitoring of proton beams and achieved excellent results. This study demonstrates that with precise tracking provided by all-digital PET, the accuracy of proton therapy will be significantly improved.

Professor Xie Qingguo from the Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics (WNLFO) at Huazhong University of Science and Technology, who led this experiment, enthusiastically introduced its significance to reporters. He vividly compared it to equipping the 'cancer warning machine'—digital PET—with a 'custom-made pair of glasses' on the 'cancer treatment tool'—the proton knife. This allows for real-time tracking during surgery and precise observation of the 'deep-water bomb' effect of proton beams on tumors.

Since the 1990s, research aimed at accurately determining the range and dose delivery of protons has not made significant breakthroughs; however, global scientists have continued their arduous efforts in this field. Meanwhile, Chinese scientists invented all-digital PET, akin to a new force equipped with advanced weaponry, embarking on this challenging journey. Professor Xie Qingguo explained that the proton monitoring 'digital PET' system based on plug-and-play imaging platforms has achieved two major breakthroughs by eliminating analog circuits: full digitalization and precise sampling. Compared to currently widely used traditional analog-digital PET systems, it not only achieves higher image resolution and sensitivity but also offers revolutionary advantages such as superior performance, ease of use, and rapid manufacturing.

The experimental results showed that 'digital PET' successfully detected oxygen-15 produced when proton beams hit human tissue for the first time. Professor Nicola, an Italian member of Xie Qingguo's team, noted that due to oxygen-15's half-life being only two minutes, using PET for online monitoring of oxygen-15 during proton beam therapy imposes extremely stringent requirements on system performance. This experiment fully validated digital PET’s outstanding capabilities: higher sensitivity and resolution, enabling the acquisition of more accurate raw data. 'Using digital PET, we can monitor the trajectory and action process of proton beams within the human body—a rare achievement!' Nicola exclaimed with excitement.

Detecting how proton beams behave inside the human body during therapy is a challenge in proton therapy. Proton beams are highly precise treatments; however, changes in tissue and tumors during treatment make it difficult to monitor whether the proton beam remains within safe limits. Chang Gung Hospital in Taiwan is the only hospital with a proton knife on the island. Vice President Hong Zhihong of Lin-Kou Chang Gung Memorial Hospital introduced that currently, there are no precise detection techniques or methods for important information such as the actual range and dose delivered by protons after entering human tissue. Professor Xie Qingguo's invention of digital PET can help understand the interaction between proton beams and biological tissues, knowing where the beam hits and what physical processes occur, clearly defining the range and dose delivery of proton beams through biological tissues. This will provide a solid theoretical basis for proton therapy planning from its roots, potentially greatly improving the treatment effect of protons on cancer.

Proton therapy is currently an advanced cancer treatment tool internationally, utilizing the 'Bragg peak' physical characteristics of proton rays to deliver energy precisely at specific tissue depths, akin to a deep-water bomb targeting tumor cells. Compared with traditional X-ray radiotherapy techniques, proton therapy is more precise and avoids damaging healthy cells during treatment. Proton therapy has significant efficacy for treating pediatric cancers, lung cancer, liver cancer, pancreatic cancer, breast cancer, prostate cancer, and nasopharyngeal carcinoma.

It's worth noting that proton therapy is one of the few 'astronomically priced surgeries' currently available; a single session in China typically costs over 200,000 RMB, while in the U.S., it ranges from $110,000 to $140,000. However, for certain specific types of tumors, such as head and neck cancers or lung cancer, treatment outcomes have not met expectations.

Two days of debugging time once again verified the unique advantages of digital PET. Professor Nicola introduced that he and his doctoral student Gao Min spent an entire night before departure disassembling and packing the all-digital PET system they had built. Upon arrival at Chang Gung Hospital, it took them only two days to set up, test, and fully debug the equipment for experimental use. 'Typically, setting up a conventional PET system is measured in years; we did it within hours. The revolutionary changes brought about by digital PET are truly unbelievable,' Nicola marveled.

Gao Min, a key member of Professor Xie Qingguo's team and winner of the 2017 Geneva International Exhibition Gold Award, despite her petite frame, is an indomitable woman. 'This time in Taiwan, Professor Nicola and I carried 80 kilograms of equipment. We literally dragged and carried it all. The night before departure, we spent a whole night disassembling and packing the equipment; upon arrival in Taiwan, we spent another night assembling it. After setup, when the equipment didn't work properly, we had to find problems one by one and conduct testsovernight. We were incredibly anxious!' Gao Min introduced that due to high startup costs for proton therapy machines, 'time is money, and we must test the equipment as quickly as possible without wasting a single second of its operational time. We accomplished this task excellently!' Speaking of which, a determined yet proud expression appeared on Gao Min's young face.

Vice President Hong Zhihong of Lin-Kou Chang Gung Memorial Hospital introduced that Chang Gung Hospital is the largest cancer treatment system in Taiwan, and its Proton & Radiotherapy Center is one of Asia’s largest and most advanced radiotherapy centers. Professor Nicola said that Chang Gung Hospital's radiation therapy hardware equipment is very advanced, with experts at the hospital having high professional levels. 'To support this experiment, the hospital reduced the number of patients treated by proton therapy machines, providing us with this expensive equipment for scientific research. This seemingly impractical action reflects a genuine commitment to humanity’s future in conquering cancer sooner!'

This experiment opened up new applications for digital PET in proton therapy. Professor Xie Qingguo stated that the success of this experiment also marked a new direction for the application of digital PET in proton therapy. In the future, from biomedical research tools to clinical medical imaging methods; from major disease diagnosis and treatment to national health monitoring management, the application of digital PET will be ubiquitous; additionally, digital PET technology will integrate with various medical devices, achieving widespread technological applications. 'It's like transforming a large device that requires dedicated operators into a universal accessory that can be placed on any equipment for simple and convenient operation. From being the main character to becoming an auxiliary part, this greatly expands the application scope of digital PET, benefiting human health!' Professor Xie Qingguo said with great anticipation.
