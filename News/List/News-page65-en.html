■Latest Discoveries and Innovations

Science and Technology Daily, Wuhan April 27 (Reporter Liu Zhiwei, Correspondent Wang Xiaoxiao) A full-body scan for a patient only takes five minutes, with the time required being about half of that needed by traditional equipment. The world's first 'full digital positron emission tomography (PET)' machine suitable for human clinical use has been successfully developed at the Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics. Its core indicators such as spatial resolution are twice as good as those of international counterparts.

Without precise detection, there can be no precision medicine, and precise detection primarily relies on imaging medical tests and genetic testing. Positron emission tomography (PET) is one of the cutting-edge molecular imaging devices in current medical applications, with significant advantages in early cancer detection among other areas. According to Professor Xie Qingguo from the Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics at Huazhong University of Science and Technology's School of Life Sciences, who led this achievement, the first human clinical 'full digital PET' consists of over 300 full digital PET detection modules. Each module uses advanced scintillation crystals and new photomultiplier devices. By leveraging full digital sampling and signal processing algorithms, its spatial resolution has reached 2.2 millimeters. This machine perfectly embodies what a full digital PET is with the characteristics of 'full digital' and 'precise sampling.' Once it enters clinical use, it will significantly enhance hospitals' ability to serve patients and reduce the cost of PET examinations.

The fully domestically innovated full digital PET has become an inevitable trend in the industry. International giants such as Germany's Siemens, Netherlands' Philips, and America's General Electric have already followed suit by launching or planning their own digital PET products. The successful development of the first human clinical 'full digital PET' marks a breakthrough in China’s independent research and development in high-end medical equipment, achieving an innovative advantage in related fields of next-generation medical imaging technology. Relevant core intellectual property patents have been completed, which will help break the current monopoly by imported high-end medical equipment and achieve a leapfrog industrial upgrading.
