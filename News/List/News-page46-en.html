The digital PET team independently developed an engineering sample of the silicon photomultiplier (SiPM)

Xinhua News Agency Hubei News, December 27, 2023 (Ma Furong, Wang Xiaoxiao): Recently, researchers from the University of Pisa published a review paper on the application of new optoelectronic conversion devices, silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs), in medical fields. The article points out that SiPMs based on standard CMOS process integrated on-chip microelectronics circuits developed by Huazhong University of Science and Technology represent the direction for the next generation; once successfully applied, it will significantly advance medicine based on increasingly quantitative medical imaging for early diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment.

The corresponding author of this article, Professor Alberto from the Department of Medical Physics at the University of Pisa, believes that new fully digital silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs) will be a leading direction in optoelectronic device technology development in the future.

The new fully digital silicon photomultiplier was developed by Professor Xie Qingguo from Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics and Huazhong University of Science and Technology, who led his team. The next generation SiPM adopts a sampling method invented by the digital PET team, solving the bottleneck issue of multi-channel data readout in analog silicon photomultipliers. This research has been continuously supported since 2008 by multiple projects from China's Ministry of Science and Technology and National Natural Science Foundation.

Photodetectors are core components in weak light detection systems and are widely used in security, industry, medical, defense, and other industries. Although silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs), commonly used optoelectronic devices internationally, can measure individual visible photons, their development has been constrained due to high integration and multi-channel data readout characteristics.

PET (Positron Emission Tomography) is a cutting-edge medical imaging technology following ultrasound, CT, and magnetic resonance imaging. It is mainly applied in the diagnosis of major diseases such as cancer, cardiovascular disease, neurological disorders, etc. Since 2006, including Xie Qingguo's team, multiple research teams worldwide have successively conducted studies on applying silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs) as core devices in PET medical imaging.

After more than a decade, Professor Xie Qingguo's team developed the fully digital silicon photomultiplier using their own sampling method. The chip array of this device is only the size of a fingernail and has features such as sub-nanosecond response speed, single-photon sensitivity, and low noise, solving the bottleneck issues of multi-channel data readout in silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs), enabling high-sensitivity full digital optoelectronic conversion.

Professor Xie Qingguo introduced that the PET imaging process involves five signal flow stages: matter to energy, high-energy particles to visible light, light to electricity, analog to digital, and data to image. The fully digital silicon photomultiplier can achieve more precise quantification in the light-to-electricity stage, meaning that PET imaging not only visualizes but also provides accurate quantitative biochemical and metabolic data, bringing revolutionary changes to disease diagnosis and pathological research.

Professor Xie Qingguo stated that the fully digital silicon photomultiplier has opened up a new frontier in optoelectronic detection technology, potentially changing China's long-term situation of being monopolized by high-performance optoelectronics technology and relying on imported components. This is significant for achieving independent innovation in weak light optoelectronic detection technology in China and enhancing the country's industrial core competitiveness.
