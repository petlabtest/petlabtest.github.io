Equip "two bombs and one star" for a healthy Chinese dream

The Apocalypse of Innovation mentions cancer, and people are always full of fear...
2013-06-20
Digital PET Laboratory

Innovative Inspirations

When it comes to cancer, people are always filled with fear. This human 'killer', due to its high mortality rate, is often spoken of in hushed tones. The National Cancer Registration Center reported in the '2012 China Cancer Registration Annual Report' that there are approximately 3.5 million new cases of cancer and about 2.5 million deaths from cancer each year in China, making cancer the number one public enemy hindering the realization of our country's health dream. The medical community has reached a consensus on 'early detection, early treatment, and early recovery' for cancer treatment. The importance of detecting cancer cells as early as possible for curing cancer cannot be overstated.

In recent decades, using advanced medical instruments such as positron emission tomography (PET), to detect and image cancer cells in the early stages represents the highest level of human detection, diagnosis, and guidance for cancer treatment. Due to technological barriers, currently, only three multinational companies—General Electric from the United States, Siemens from Germany, and Philips from the Netherlands—are independently capable of researching and producing PET machines globally. All PET machines used in China are imported. As a typical large medical device, PET represents the highest level of development in a country's medical equipment industry, comparable to the 'Two Bombs, One Satellite' program in the medical equipment sector. However, digitalizing PET instruments has always been a world-class challenge and an objective for scientists and industries around the globe. Under the support of the National Natural Science Foundation of China, the Ministry of Science and Technology, and the Ministry of Education, Professor Xie Qingguo from the Department of Biomedical Photonics at Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics (WNLFO) led a multidisciplinary team of 13 disciplines in collaborative innovation between industry, academia, and research over more than ten years, successfully overcoming this world-class challenge.

Research Chose Me

Before choosing to develop digital PET, Xie Qingguo did not know where his future would lead.

In 2001, at the age of 29, Xie Qingguo graduated from Huazhong University of Science and Technology (HUST) with a doctorate in control engineering and control theory. However, after that, he went through a long period of confusion. Faced with numerous choices, he did not know how to plan his future. He often spent an entire afternoon playing basketball until he was exhausted. His classmates called him 'the laziest person in HUST's Department of Automation', but he just smiled it off.

In that era when material conditions were scarce, many college graduates chose 'golden rice bowls' or 'iron rice bowls' as their employment options. However, Xie Qingguo did not want to follow his classmates and choose a job that was merely for survival and comfort. Under the influence of HUST's strong academic research atmosphere, Xie Qingguo firmly believed that his true path should be like what great scientist Stephen Hawking said: doing work he dreams about and being recognized and affirmed for it.

So, 'engaging in scientific research, a job that combines theory with practical application and integrates the essence of human wisdom, chose me,' Xie Qingguo said with a sense of mission.

Xie Qingguo believes that research is not just about discussing lofty theories. He constantly reminds himself that what he studies are not empty formulas but weapons to change people's lives. This belief has been unwavering since he chose the path of scientific research and eventually succeeded in developing the world's first digital PET.

A String of Numbers Begins Research

From choosing to engage in research to deciding to develop a digital PET, Xie Qingguo’s decision was based on a string of numbers.

'China has become the country with the highest incidence rate of cancer. More than 4000 people die from cancer every day, and one out of seven or eight people dies from cancer. It is predicted that by 2030, China will have over 4.86 million new cases and 3.6 million deaths due to cancer.'

Such shocking numbers woke up Xie Qingguo who was then pursuing his dream in research.

Humans have been fighting against cancer since the beginning of time, but when would there be a 'preemptive strike' weapon that could eliminate cancer cells at their inception? After obtaining his doctorate, Xie Qingguo went to Taiwan's Institute of Health and Sanitation Research, the University of Chicago, and Argonne National Laboratory in the United States for research and study. During this period, he learned a lot about medical engineering, radiology, and high-energy physics. At that time, he realized that PET (positron emission tomography) was the weapon he had been looking for, one of the best means for early clinical diagnosis and guiding cancer treatment, but due to technical bottlenecks in digitizing ultra-high-speed scintillation pulse signals, there were only analog and hybrid analog-digital machines available, limiting their functionality.

Why not digitalize PET? Xie Qingguo boldly proposed this idea. At that time, a wave of digitalization was sweeping the globe, with digital technology gradually penetrating into medical imaging fields. However, digitizing PET remained a world-class challenge, an objective for scientists around the world to tackle. In the field of high-end medical equipment digitization, China's presence was still blank.

This dream that seemed 'impossible' to outsiders, Xie Qingguo resolved to make it happen. Beyond this, he made an even more difficult decision—returning home from scratch. He told himself: The world’s first digital PET must be born in China.

Starting Over, Innovation Through Collaboration

In 2004, upon returning to his alma mater Huazhong University of Science and Technology (HUST), Xie Qingguo established the PET Instrument Development and Multi-Modality Medical Imaging Laboratory, gradually gathering a multidisciplinary team of medical-physics integration and medical-engineering crossover. At the beginning of research, single-handedly tackling challenges, Xie Qingguo encountered many difficulties. As someone from an engineering background, he did not know how to take the first step into the high-end medical equipment industry.

After multiple efforts, Xie Qingguo found Zhang Yongxue at Huazhong University of Science and Technology Union Hospital's Nuclear Medicine Department. Initially, basic communication between their two teams was extremely difficult due to different disciplinary backgrounds and research areas leading to distinct language systems.

To break down barriers, they often 'wrapped' themselves together. Xie Qingguo said, 'Like two pieces of dough, only by actively applying force can they be kneaded into one.' For the sake of research, they continuously learned from each other, often holding seminars to discuss various issues. Each meeting was a learning process.

To facilitate better experiments, Xie Qingguo sent his most capable students to Huazhong University of Science and Technology Union Medical College for practical experience, grounding them in medical practice. Traveling between Wuchang and Hankou by bus took four hours each way; initially, the students complained about it. However, Xie Qingguo comforted them, saying, 'You are doing what you love every day, fighting for your dreams—how fortunate! What's a little hardship?'

The Dream Is Still Far Away

After more than ten years of trials and overcoming technical challenges, in 2012, Xie Qingguo successfully developed the digital PET, which is like the 'Two Bombs, One Satellite' program for medical equipment, born as expected in China.

At a results appraisal meeting held this April, an expert panel led by Academician Yu Mengsun of the Chinese Academy of Engineering gave the digital PET the highest evaluation. The appraisal concluded that the technology reached international leading levels. This breakthrough by Chinese scientists in PET digitization means that earlier and more sensitive detection of tumors and diagnosis of cancer will no longer be a dream. At the appraisal meeting, Xie Qingguo told the expert panel, 'This instrument carries not only our invention team's dreams but also the responsibility of researchers to society and their care for human health.'

However, after receiving recognition, Xie Qingguo felt that his dream was still far away.

'We have collaborated with Suzhou Rayence Technology Co., Ltd. to develop a small digital PET machine which has been successfully applied. Currently, we are planning to make a whole-body clinical PET prototype and conduct clinical trials. Next, I hope our independently owned digital PET can truly benefit humanity and serve society.'

Xie Qingguo told reporters that currently there are about 5000 commercial PET machines globally, nearly 3000 of which are in the United States. China has fewer than 200 clinical-use machines, all imported. However, now Xie's team owns complete independent intellectual property rights for digital PET. If they can industrialize China’s independently developed clinical digital PET, it will not only benefit a large number of patients but also break Western companies' monopoly and achieve zero breakthroughs in our country's high-end medical equipment industry.
