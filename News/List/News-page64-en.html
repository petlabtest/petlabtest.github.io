World’s First Human Whole-Body All-Digital PET Debuts in Wuhan: China’s Intelligent Manufacturing of High-End Medical Instruments

CGTN News, April 28, Wuhan (Reporter Ling Shu, Communicator Wang Xiaoxiao) The first 'fully digital positron emission tomography (PET)' machine suitable for human clinical use has recently been successfully developed at the National Laboratory of Optoelectronics in Wuhan. Its core indicators such as spatial resolution are twice as good as those of international counterparts.

As an advanced imaging technique in nuclear medicine, PET technology itself already has significant advantages in early cancer detection. The 'fully digital positron emission tomography (PET)' derived from principle innovation differs fundamentally from traditional PET in terms of research and development approach. This achievement originates from the first national major scientific instrument equipment development project at Huazhong University of Science and Technology, titled 'Development and Application of Ultra-High Resolution PET'. It was completed by a team led by Professor Xie Qingguo, researcher at the National Laboratory of Optoelectronics (provisional) and professor at the School of Life Sciences, Huazhong University of Science and Technology.

The characteristics of 'full digital' and 'precise sampling' make it more accurate than traditional PET in detection precision and broader in development prospects. Professor Xie Qingguo, who leads this project, introduced that the first human clinical 'fully digital PET' consists of over 300 fully digital PET detector modules, each using advanced scintillation crystals and new photomultiplier devices. By leveraging full digital sampling and signal processing algorithms, its spatial resolution has reached 2.2 millimeters, nearly doubling the highest level currently available in imported equipment.

The first human clinical 'fully digital PET' requires only five minutes for a whole-body scan on patients, taking about half the time of traditional devices, achieving both speed and accuracy in fully digital PET detection. Once this technology enters clinical application, it will significantly enhance hospitals' capacity to serve patients and reduce the cost of PET examinations.

Previously, China's PET market was largely dominated by international giants such as Germany's Siemens, Netherlands' Philips, and America's General Electric. A single PET examination could cost a patient tens of thousands of yuan or more. The successful development of the first human clinical 'fully digital PET' is expected to provide Chinese patients with higher quality and lower-cost medical detection services; it also marks China's breakthrough in independent research and development in high-end medical equipment, gaining an innovative advantage in related fields of next-generation medical imaging technology. Relevant core intellectual property patents have been completed, which will help break the current monopoly by imported high-end medical instruments and achieve a leapfrog upgrade in the industry.
