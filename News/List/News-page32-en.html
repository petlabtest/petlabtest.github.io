Announcement of the 2019 Top 10 Science and Technology Developments in China and the World by Academicians of the Two Academies

Sponsored by the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the Chinese Academy of Engineering, and hosted by the Work Bureau of the Faculty of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, the General Office of the Chinese Academy of Engineering, the Chinese Science Newspaper, and voted by the academicians of the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the Chinese Academy of Engineering, the 2019 Top Ten Science and Technology Progress News and the World Top Ten Science and Technology Progress News were announced in Beijing on January 11...
2021-01-25

The Top Ten National and International Scientific and Technological Advances of the Year 2019, organized by the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS) and the Chinese Academy of Engineering (CAE), were announced in Beijing on January 11. The event was hosted by the Bureau of Academic Affairs of CAS and the Office of CAE, with the support of Science and Technology Daily. The awards were selected through voting by academicians from both institutions.


This annual selection activity has been held for 26 consecutive years.


After extensive media coverage of the results, it generated a strong social response, further informing the public about the dynamics of domestic and international scientific development, playing a positive role in promoting and popularizing science and technology.




The Top Ten National Scientific and Technological Advances of 2019




1. Chang'e-4 Achieves First Soft Landing on Lunar Far Side



On January 3, 2019, the Chang'e-4 probe successfully landed in the Von Kármán crater within the South Pole-Aitken basin on the far side of the moon, with support from the Queqiao relay satellite. The Chang'e-4 lander and Yutu-2 rover then conducted in-situ exploration and roving reconnaissance respectively.

The work duration of Chang'e-4 on the lunar surface has exceeded 300 days, far surpassing its designed lifespan; the Yutu-2 rover overcame various challenges to travel more than 300 meters, achieving a 'double three hundred' breakthrough.


Based on the data obtained from scientific payloads, the research team reconstructed the descent trajectory of Chang'e-4 on the far side of the moon and precisely located the landing site; they acquired geological information such as morphology, structure, and composition of the landing area, discovered rocks mainly composed of olivine and low-calcium pyroxene minerals, and made preliminary judgments about their origins. This has significant value for revealing the geological evolution history of the South Pole-Aitken basin, early lunar crustal evolution, deep lunar material structure, and formation mechanisms; additionally, a large amount of data was collected in fields such as neutral atoms, neutron and radiation dose on the lunar surface, and low-frequency radio spectra, with preliminary analysis results achieved.


Chinese astronomers have discovered the largest stellar black hole to date.



On November 28, 2019, an international scientific journal Nature published a significant discovery by a research team from the National Astronomical Observatories of the Chinese Academy of Sciences led by Liu Jifeng and Zhang Haotong.


Relying on China's independently developed major national science and technology infrastructure, the LAMOST (Guo Shoujing Telescope), the research team discovered the largest stellar black hole to date with a mass of 70 times that of the Sun and provided a new method for finding black holes using the survey advantages of LAMOST.


This 70-solar-mass black hole far exceeds the theoretical upper limit of stellar black hole masses, overturning people's understanding of the formation of stellar black holes and is expected to drive innovation in theories of stellar evolution and black hole formation.


It is reported that based on the discovery of the largest stellar black hole to date, the research team plans to implement a 'Black Hole Hunter' program for their next phase of work, aiming to discover and measure nearly 100 black holes over the next five years.


Chinese scientists have observed three-dimensional quantum Hall effect for the first time.



The Hall effect describes the relationship between electric force and magnetic force when a magnetic field is applied to metals and semiconductors. Over nearly 140 years, international scientific circles have successively discovered the Hall effect and the quantum Hall effect.


A research team from Professor Qiao Zhenhua's group at the University of Science and Technology of China collaborated with Professor Zhang Liyuan's group at Southern University of Science and Technology, among others, to first achieve something in millimeter-sized zirconium telluride (PET)ZrTe5) Clear evidence of the three-dimensional quantum Hall effect has been observed in bulk single-crystal materials, and it is suggested that this effect may be induced by charge density waves caused by interactions under a magnetic field. This significant research achievement was published online on May 9, 2019, in the renowned academic journal Nature.


It is reported that since the discovery of the quantum Hall effect in 1980, the academic community has focused its attention on two-dimensional systems. The discovery of this three-dimensional quantum Hall effect completes an important piece of the puzzle in the family of Hall effects.


4. Chinese scientists have developed a new brain-inspired computing chip



After years of effort, Chinese scientists successfully developed a novel brain-inspired computing chip for artificial general intelligence—named the 'Tianjic' chip—and it was also tested on an unmanned bicycle.


Professor Luping Shi's team from Tsinghua University's Brain-Inspired Computing Research Center published their related paper titled 'Heterogeneous 'Tianjic' Chip Architecture for Artificial General Intelligence' in the international journal Nature as a cover article on August 1, 2019.


It is reported that the first and second generation of Tianjic chips were successfully developed in 2015 and 2017 respectively. After continuous improvements to its design, the current second-generation Tianjic chip features high speed, high performance, and low power consumption.


The future direction of the 'Tianji Chip' is to provide a high-efficiency, fast, and flexible computing platform for research on general artificial intelligence (AI), which can also be used in various application developments to promote AI research and empower all industries.


5. World's First 100 MW Hydroelectric Unit Core Component Completed and Delivered



The runner of the first 100 MW hydro-generator unit at Baihetan Hydropower Station, developed and manufactured by Dongfang Electric Corporation Limited (DEC), was officially completed on January 12, 2019, in the rotor workshop at the power station site ahead of schedule.


This will effectively ensure that Baihetan Hydropower Station achieves its goal of putting the first unit into operation as scheduled in 2021.


It is reported that a 100 MW hydro-generator set is currently the largest single-unit capacity machine in the world, and it represents China's push towards the 'uncharted territory' of global hydropower. The most critical and challenging component to manufacture within the entire unit is the runner, which can be considered the heart of the generator. Weighing a total of 353 tons, the runner went through 24 processes and was meticulously crafted by over 100 DEC craftsmen in just one year. The design and development of the entire 100 MW unit took ten years.


6. 'Taiji One' In-Orbit Testing Successful; China Takes First Step Toward Space-Based Gravitational Wave Detection



China's first experimental satellite for space-based gravitational wave detection has successfully completed its first phase of in-orbit testing tasks.


On September 20, 2019, the satellite was named 'Taiji One'. In space, it can detect signals from mid-to-low frequency gravitational waves. However, due to the extremely weak nature of these signals, it requires breaking through the current limits of human precision measurement and control technology.


A research team from the Chinese Academy of Sciences completed the development task of 'Taiji-1' in less than a year.


The results of the first phase on-orbit testing show that the laser interferometer displacement measurement accuracy reached the picometer level, approximately one atom's size; the gravitational reference sensor measurement accuracy achieved one billionth of Earth's gravity acceleration, equivalent to the acceleration generated when an ant pushes 'Taiji-1'; and the micro-thruster thrust resolution reached sub-micro-newton levels, roughly one ten-thousandth of a sesame seed's weight. China has taken its first foundational step in space-based gravitational wave detection.


The latest research indicates that approximately 24% of materials in nature may possess topological structures



On February 28, 2019, at dawn, three research groups from the Institute of Physics of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Nanjing University, and Princeton University published their latest related research findings in the journal Nature.


Their studies show that thousands of known materials may possess topological properties, meaning approximately 24% of materials in nature could have such structures. Topology describes the properties of geometric figures or spaces that remain unchanged under continuous deformation.


When the mathematical concept 'topology' was introduced into physics, it not only promoted the development of fundamental physics research but also led to the emergence of numerous novel topological materials.


Chinese scientists unravel new light-capturing mechanism in 'exotic' photosynthetic species diatoms




A recent study by the Institute of Botany at the Chinese Academy of Sciences discovered how an exotic photosynthetic organism, diatoms, utilize their unique structures to efficiently capture and use light energy. On February 8, 2019, the renowned academic journal Science published this research in its long-form article section.


Based on this study, scientists may design new crops capable of efficient 'light capture' in the future. The team led by Professor Jianren Shen and Professor Tingyun Kuang from the Institute of Botany at the Chinese Academy of Sciences has elucidated the high-resolution structure of diatoms' main light-harvesting antenna proteins, marking the first structural analysis of a photosynthetic membrane protein in diatoms. This provides an important structural basis for studying diatom's mechanisms of light energy capture, utilization, and photoprotection.


China's independently developed clinical full-digital PET/CT equipment has been approved to enter the market



Professor Qingguo Xie from Huazhong University of Science and Technology's team invented the full digital PET/CT. It was registered with China’s National Medical Products Administration on May 31, 2019, obtaining market entry and sales qualifications. This marks a significant breakthrough in China's high-end medical equipment development by breaking international technological monopolies.


PET stands for Positron Emission Tomography, which is the latest advanced medical imaging technology following ultrasound, CT, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). It has immense application value in early diagnosis, treatment evaluation, and pathological research of major diseases such as malignant tumors, neurological disorders, cardiovascular diseases, etc.


Scientists discover 160,000-year-old Denisovan jawbone fossil



Chinese scientists, along with multiple domestic and international researchers, published an article on May 2, 2019, in the journal Nature. They reported finding a human jawbone fossil at the Bai Shiyi Cave in Xiahe County, Gansu Province, China, located at an altitude of 3,280 meters above sea level on the northeastern Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. The fossil was identified as belonging to a Denisovan individual.


This research pushes back the earliest known human activity on the Tibetan Plateau from around 40,000 years ago to approximately 160,000 years ago, advancing by an entire 120,000 years.


The research team led by Academician Fahu Chen of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau Institute of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Associate Professor Dongju Zhang from Lanzhou University's School of Earth and Environmental Science, and Professor Jean-Jacques Hublin from the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Germany conducted nearly a decade of systematic archaeological surveys at the fossil discovery site and its surroundings. They discovered numerous cultural remains that may have coexisted with this fossil, providing critical information for further research on Denisovan culture, behavior characteristics, and high-altitude environmental adaptation strategies.




Top Ten Global Scientific Advances of 2019




Humanity Sees Its First Image of a Black Hole



On April 10, 2019, the Event Horizon Telescope project, involving hundreds of researchers worldwide, held simultaneous press conferences in multiple locations to release the first image of a black hole.


The 'star' of the photo is the supermassive black hole at the center of the giant elliptical galaxy Messier 87 (M87) in the Virgo constellation, with a mass equivalent to 6.5 billion suns and located approximately 55 million light-years from Earth.


The image shows a bright ring-shaped structure centered on a dark region, resembling a donut; the black part is the shadow cast by the black hole, while the bright portion represents the accretion disk rotating around it at high speed.


This photograph provides direct evidence of the existence of black holes and verifies general relativity. It will also help answer many frontier questions about how spectacular jets in galaxies are produced and their impact on galaxy evolution.


DNA Microscope Developed



The Howard Hughes Medical Institute and Broad Institute jointly developed a 'DNA microscope', a new cellular visualization technology that uses chemical methods to obtain internal cell information, mapping images reflecting the genetic sequences and relative positions of biomolecules within cells.


This research was published in the journal Cell on June 20, 2019.


It is reported that DNA microscopes can perform tasks beyond those of optical microscopes. For example, optical microscopes often cannot distinguish between cells with different DNA, such as immune cells; by identifying immune cells capable of attacking tumors, DNA microscopes may help improve the treatment of certain cancers.


Hayabusa 2 Successfully Lands on Asteroid 'Ryugu' and Collects Samples



The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency announced on February 22, 2019, that based on received data, Hayabusa 2 successfully landed on asteroid Ryugu at 7:48 AM local time (6:48 AM Beijing Time) to collect samples before briefly resting and taking off again.


Hayabusa 2 was launched from the Uchinoura Space Center in Kagoshima Prefecture, Japan, on December 3, 2014. After a journey of about three and a half years through space, it arrived near asteroid Ryugu on June 27, 2018. It is scheduled to remain near Ryugu for approximately one and a half years before returning to Earth by the end of 2020.


Google Researchers Announce Successful Demonstration of 'Quantum Advantage'



"Quantum Supremacy" is used to describe a critical milestone in the development of quantum computers, referring to the ability of quantum computers to solve certain special problems that traditional computers cannot resolve within a reasonable timeframe.


Achieving this goal requires overcoming many challenges, including ensuring low error rates while generating larger computational spaces and designing benchmark tests that are difficult for traditional computers but easy for quantum computers to complete.


On October 23, 2019, researchers from Google published a paper in the British journal Nature announcing they had successfully demonstrated "Quantum Supremacy," with a quantum system completing tasks that would take traditional supercomputers several days within about 200 seconds.


5. Scientists Synthesize World's First Pure Carbon Ring Containing 18 Carbon Atoms



On August 15, 2019, the journal Science published a collaborative achievement from Oxford University's Department of Chemistry and IBM's Zurich Research Laboratory; they synthesized the world's first ring-shaped molecule entirely composed of carbon atoms—C18, with 18 carbon atoms connected by alternating single bonds and triple bonds. Early research found thatC18ring molecules exhibit semiconductor properties, indicating that similar carbon straight-chain structures may serve as molecular-level electronic components.


The research team will continue to study the basic properties, including stability, of the obtainedC18molecule.


6. Novel Artificial DNA Structure Doubles Information Density

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) stores genetic codes and is composed of four nucleotides represented by four different letters. American researchers have recently synthesized a new DNA structure with eight letters, doubling information storage density, which may be applied in synthetic biology fields.


In February 2019, the research team led by Steven Benner from the Foundation for Applied Molecular Evolution published a report in Science stating that their newly synthesized DNA molecular system differs significantly from natural DNA in having eight rather than four life-information components.


Besides including the four natural nucleotides such as adenine, it also contains another four artificial information units with similar structures, forming a double helix structure capable of storing and transmitting information.


7. Human Physiological Age Successfully Reversed for First Time



A small clinical study conducted in California has shown for the first time that reversing human epigenetic biological clocks is possible. The epigenetic biological clock can measure an individual's physiological age.


Over a period of one year, nine healthy volunteers took three common drugs—growth hormone and two diabetes medications. By analyzing markers on the human genome, researchers found that the average physiological age of these participants decreased by 2.5 years.


At the same time, signs of rejuvenation were observed in their immune systems. The study results were published in Aging Cell on September 5, 2019.


8. HIV Treatment Miracle Repeated: 'London Patient' May Be Cured



On March 5, 2019, the journal Nature published a paper stating that an HIV patient known as the 'London Patient,' after stem cell transplantation treatment, had not detected HIV for 18 months.


He may become the second person to be cured of AIDS following the 'Berlin Patient,' but experts cautiously note that continued monitoring is needed. Both patients underwent radiotherapy and chemotherapy to treat cancer, which might also help eliminate HIV; however, both treatments have side effects.


Compared with the Berlin patient's full-body radiation therapy, the London patient received relatively mild chemotherapy. Researchers believe that the experience of the 'London Patient' may be more widely applicable.


9. Scientists Cultivate New E. coli That Can Feed on Carbon Dioxide



On November 28, 2019, a new study published in Cell reported that scientists from Israel's Weizmann Institute of Science have modified a bacterium that typically feeds on simple sugars to enable it to build cells like plants by absorbing carbon dioxide.


It is known that researchers added an enzyme for converting carbon dioxide and removed other enzymes used for sugar metabolism, successfully altering the 'food' source they rely on. To prove these bacteria do not need sugar to survive, scientists placed them in a laboratory environment for 200 days.


When re-examining these bacteria, researchers found that they had successfully 'evolved,' and could grow without sugar. This achievement opens up exciting new prospects for using engineered bacteria to convert waste products into fuel, food, or other valuable compounds.


10. World's First Ebola Vaccine Approved by EU



On November 12, 2019, European regulators approved a vaccine that has already helped control deadly outbreaks of the Ebola virus—this is the first immunization against Ebola to pass this review. The Ebola virus is a highly infectious disease-causing virus primarily transmitted through bodily fluids and can cause fatal hemorrhagic fever.


Previously, medical researchers have invested significant effort in developing Ebola vaccines, but most remained at the clinical trial stage; Ervebo became the first officially approved vaccine for human use against Ebola. Merck has also submitted an application to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), with the vaccine expected to be approved for sale in the United States by the first quarter of 2020.
