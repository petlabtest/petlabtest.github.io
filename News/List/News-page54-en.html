"Digital PET" received special approval from the National Innovation Medical Device

Wuhan, Jan...
2018-01-30
People’s Daily Online

People's Daily Wuhan, January 29th: It was recently learned that Professor Xie Qingguo from the National Optoelectronics Research Center in Wuhan and a professor at Huazhong University of Science and Technology’s School of Life Sciences has invented high-end medical equipment 'Digital PET', which has received special approval for innovative medical devices by the state. This 'special approval' means that the state has opened up a 'green channel' for Digital PET to enter clinical applications, breaking the monopoly of imported high-end medical instruments in China's PET market.

On January 10th, 2018, the National Medical Products Administration’s Center for Medical Device Evaluation released the Notice on the Review Results of Applications for Special Approval of Innovative Medical Devices (No. 1 of 2018). Four products including 'Fully Digital Positron Emission and X-ray Tomography Imaging System' passed the approval process and completed public notice on January 23rd.

PET is an important device in 'precision medicine', alongside CT and MRI, known as the three major components of medical imaging. It serves as a powerful tool for early diagnosis of cancer and other serious diseases. However, due to limitations imposed by ultra-high-speed scintillation pulse signal precise digitalization, PET has long suffered from the shortcomings of being difficult to use and having limited applications; additionally, the high technical threshold for PET development has been monopolized by Western multinational giants for a long time, with prices remaining prohibitively high, becoming a 'pain point' in China's healthcare sector.

The Digital PET technology is characterized fundamentally by its 'full digitalization' and 'precise sampling'. It obtains precise source signal data without the use of analog circuits, thus possessing revolutionary advantages such as 'superior performance, ease of use, and rapid manufacturing'. Under the support of national ministries including the National Natural Science Foundation Committee, Ministry of Science and Technology, and Ministry of Education, Professor Xie Qingguo led his team through eighteen years of arduous efforts to develop a complete set of independent intellectual property rights for digital PET. The clinical full-digital PET was produced as a significant outcome under the support of the National Major Scientific Instrument Development Program 'Development and Application of Ultra-High Resolution PET'.

Professor Xie Qingguo from the Wuhan National Optoelectronics Research Center and Huazhong University of Science and Technology’s School of Life Sciences, who invented Digital PET, believes: 'Digital PET, as an innovation from fundamental scientific principles to globally leading clinical equipment development, fully meets all requirements for innovative medical devices. Being included in the special approval process for innovative medical devices is like walking through a green channel; government departments will accelerate and improve processes from top-level design to review and approval stages, significantly shortening the time it takes for Digital PET to be put into clinical use and benefit patients.'

The 'Special Approval Procedure for Innovative Medical Devices (Trial)' released by the National Food and Drug Administration in 2014 stipulates that innovative products with Chinese invention patents, possessing independent intellectual property rights, being domestically original technologically and reaching international leading levels, and having significant clinical application value will be included in special approval channels, receiving priority technical review and administrative approval. According to this procedure, for the registration of innovative medical devices, from submission to completion of registration, it may only take 60 working days, a substantial reduction compared to previous approval times. Since the implementation of the innovation approval process, companies have been enthusiastic in applying, but due to high thresholds and strict requirements, only 159 products have been approved since 2014.

Zhang Bo, the person in charge of industrialization for clinical fully digital PET/CT, stated, “From what we can see now, our clinical trials at two hospitals in Guangzhou have yielded good results. Being able to enter the national special approval 'green channel' this time means that the completion and market entry period for clinical fully digital PET will be shortened by at least half a year!”

In 2015, the government of Ezhou City, Hubei Province introduced digital PET technology and established an industrial entity—Hubei Ruisi Digital Medical Imaging Technology Co., Ltd. to develop the world's first large clinical fully digital PET/CT. Its core indicators are comprehensively leading in international similar products; its spatial resolution of 2.1 millimeters is far higher than that of similar equipment from international giants such as Siemens, Philips, and GE Healthcare, achieving an eightfold performance improvement on a voxel basis. Currently, the instrument is undergoing clinical validation work at the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University and the Cancer Hospital, and it will soon complete CFDA certification for formal mass production and market entry.

Zhang Bo introduced that there has been no breakthrough in PET technology over the past forty years since its application, with the digitalization challenge remaining unresolved. The introduction of digital PET into clinical use represents a remarkable innovation initiated by Chinese people, akin to how digital cameras replaced film cameras and smartphones replaced ordinary phones, which will have a disruptive impact on the traditional PET market.

According to the latest publicly released data from the National Central Cancer Registry, there were approximately 4.29 million new cancer cases in China in 2015, equivalent to an average of 12,000 new cases per day and 2.81 million death cases, or about 7,500 deaths due to cancer each day. New cancer cases account for nearly 22% of the global total, with 27% of cancer-related deaths occurring in China; thus, China is truly a major country affected by cancer. The five-year survival rate for Chinese cancer patients is only 30.9%, less than half that of the United States. Cancer has become the leading cause of disease mortality among Chinese citizens. Digital PET, with its outstanding imaging performance, significantly enhances early detection capabilities for cancer, meaning that cancer can be detected much earlier. For patients, this not only means a difference between tens of thousands and hundreds of thousands in treatment costs but may also mean the difference between life and death.

High-performance medical devices are one of the ten key areas supported by the 'Made in China 2025' plan. Originating innovation's 'digital PET' is a flagship product of Made in China 2025, its entry into clinical validation phase being a major demonstration of China’s comprehensive national strength breaking through in high-end medical equipment fields, indicating that the current situation where imported high-end medical devices dominate the market in a country with a heavy cancer burden may be comprehensively broken.
