Young Team Strikes Back at World Problem - China's Digital PET R&D Documentary

“Preliminary estimates are that we will produce the first large-scale digital PET prototype by the end of this year; if there is investment, the product will be launched to the public in 2 years...
2013-10-01
Digital PET Laboratory

"Our preliminary estimate is that by the end of this year, we will produce our first large-scale digital PET prototype; with investment, the product could be brought to market in two years." This is the recent goal set by Professor Xie Qingguo's team at Huazhong University of Science and Technology. Their research achievement, 'Technical Breakthroughs in Digital PET,' was selected as one of the top ten scientific progress news nominations for China in 2012 by the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Engineering.

What exactly is digital PET? Zhang Yongxue, chairman of the Nuclear Medicine Branch of the China Nuclear Society, compares it to a telescope that aids human exploration of space. 'It enables doctors to detect tumors earlier and with greater precision.' This significantly increases the chances for cancer patients to discover their condition early and start treatment sooner, thereby saving substantial medical expenses and leading to leapfrog development in China's high-end medical equipment industry.

Starting from an Iron Shed

PET stands for Positron Emission Tomography, which can quantitatively understand and evaluate diseases at the cellular metabolic level. It enables doctors to 'see' cancer non-invasively through visualization, making it one of the best methods currently used in clinical settings for diagnosing and guiding tumor treatment. It represents the highest level of development in a country's medical equipment industry. The global clinical PET market is largely dominated by three multinational companies: General Electric from the United States, Siemens from Germany, and Philips from the Netherlands, with digitalization having been a worldwide challenge since the 1980s.

In Wuhan, a beautiful city along the Yangtze River, a group of young people under the age of 30 have gathered at Huazhong University of Science and Technology's 'PET Instrument Development and Multi-Modal Medical Imaging Laboratory' under Professor Xie Qingguo's inspiration. They form a team covering 13 disciplines, integrating medical science with engineering in an atmosphere rich with academic research, driven by passion, dreams, courage, and responsibility.
First proposed the multi-voltage threshold sampling method to solve the challenge of directly digitizing high-speed PET scintillation pulses;
Conducted the first application of new silicon photomultiplier tubes in PET instruments;
Obtained the world's first digital PET invention patent;
Developed fully digital, modular PET detectors;
Produced the first small-scale digital PET machine;
……

Now, with support from the National Natural Science Foundation of China, the Ministry of Science and Technology, and the Ministry of Education, the digital PET team has been granted over twenty patents worldwide, securing complete independent intellectual property rights. It is expected that within three to five years, the total number of patents will reach 100. In terms of resolution, a critical metric, commercial PET can diagnose cancer when there are only one-fifth as many cells present compared to what digital PET can detect.

This achievement is also a result of collaborative innovation. Not only does the research team consist of researchers from thirteen disciplines, but it also collaborates with Huazhong University of Science and Technology's Tongji Medical College Affiliated Union Hospital PET Center. To overcome professional barriers between the two teams, they often 'wrap' themselves together, continuously adapting and learning from each other. Professor Xie Qingguo even sends his best students to the PET center for hands-on medical experience.

The efforts made by the digital PET team are known only to them. Outsiders may gain some insight into their philosophy of survival and development: 'Struggle for growth, grow to seek support, and achieve excellence through support.'

When recalling these arduous journeys, Professor Xie Qingguo remains serene and even self-deprecatingly humorous. The team's innovation mission is guided by social needs and personal interests, aiming to cultivate industry leaders while fully motivating each student's potential. This non-utilitarian interest and vitality enable the team to face any challenge with a positive attitude.

Letting Young People Take Charge

During interviews with the digital PET team, reporters noticed that both actual research operations and daily management were handled by young students in an orderly yet vibrant manner. In this laboratory, mentors only play a leading role when it comes to major decisions such as lab development plans and research directions. Otherwise, the lab encourages innovation without rigid rules, fully entrusting leadership and management to students.

"Stimulating students' initiative should be the cornerstone of talent cultivation." Since the establishment of the lab, Professor Xie has adhered to this teaching philosophy, adopting a modular management structure divided into three parts: research units, comprehensive project units, and research support units. All major work is completed autonomously by students. Additionally, the lab has established a unique evaluation system that guides students in setting life and career goals while fostering their sense of social responsibility.

Zhu Jun, who joined the laboratory in 2005, is now an old hand in the team and currently a doctoral student. He frequently attends academic lectures and regularly trains new members of the lab. 'In my first year at university, I entered Professor Xie's lab. After a year of deep involvement, I became increasingly convinced that digital PET research is both valuable and enjoyable, so I decided to dedicate my entire academic career to it.' Zhu Jun's words reflect the sentiments of many team members.

Another doctoral student, Yuan Rong, not only handles administrative tasks but also develops an individualized liver surgery planning system for PET/CT equipment under Professor Xie's forward-thinking guidance. This system uses medical image processing technology and patented image segmentation and modeling methods to provide 3D models of the liver, blood vessels, and lesions before surgery, assessing surgical risks and outcomes.
Professor Xie believes that students should plan their careers based on personal interests and academic backgrounds, fully leveraging each member's motivation, passion, and potential. 'One must continuously self-motivate with courage and a sense of social responsibility to achieve one's ideals.'

Promising Industrial Prospects

After obtaining his doctorate in 2001, Professor Xie Qingguo was once jokingly called the 'idle person' at Huazhong University of Science and Technology. For nine months straight, he often spent afternoons playing basketball while pondering how to plan a research field that is both valuable and widely applicable.

Under the principle that 'research should be driven by application,' the team has completed the entire R&D process for digital PET through an innovative model combining original innovation, industrial operation, and clinical application. They are now advancing towards clinical application and industrial production stages.

In 2010, a small-scale digital PET machine was unveiled after a series of animal imaging trials that yielded impressive scientific data with world-leading performance metrics. Currently, in collaboration with high-tech company Suzhou Rayence Technology Co., Ltd., the small-scale digital PET machine is about to enter the market and will play a significant role in research and drug development.

Experts estimate that if medical imaging equipment such as CT and MRI are used as references, after the innovation of digital PET, the global PET device market could increase from $8 billion to $100 billion, while the global PET application market would grow from $5 billion per year to $200 billion per year. Therefore, experts suggest that China should pay attention to and support the localization of self-innovated digital PET as a major public welfare project and emerging high-end equipment industry. This will not only benefit the people but also potentially replace existing commercial PET with digital PET, triggering a revolution in medical high-end equipment manufacturing and application.
