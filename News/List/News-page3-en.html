All-Digital PET Welcomes New Flagship with 135-cm Axial Length, Enabling Single-Bed Whole-Body Scanning

The industrialization of digital PET has achieved new progress. Recently, the clinical all-digital PET/CT (DigitMI 9120) obtained the National Class III Medical Device Registration Certificate, officially approved for market entry.
May 19, 2025
Digital PET Laboratory

The industrialization of digital PET has achieved new progress. Recently, the clinical all-digital PET/CT (DigitMI 9120) obtained the National Class III Medical Device Registration Certificate, officially approved for market entry.

Guoqing Zhang, Deputy General Manager of Raysolution, the industrialization unit for digital PET, introduced that this latest all-digital PET device, while continuing to use the original MVT (Multi-Voltage Threshold) digital sampling method, has further upgraded its hardware configuration. The 135-cm axial field of view enables complete head-to-torso scanning in a single bed position, representing the optimal cost-effective configuration for whole-body PET systems and truly the flagship product in the all-digital PET product matrix.

Thanks to its ultra-long axial length, the DigitMI 9120 can increase scanning speed by up to 50 times, achieving whole-body imaging in a single bed position in just 2 seconds—currently the fastest single-bed imaging speed globally—significantly reducing examination time. Furthermore, due to the substantial improvement in detection efficiency, the required tracer injection dose is also greatly reduced. This makes PET examinations safer for sensitive groups like children and meets the needs of cancer patients for multiple follow-ups to track treatment efficacy.

Dr. Bingxuan Li, a Digital PETer, explained that besides greatly enhancing patient experience and device safety, this equipment also achieves millisecond-level, ultra-high-frequency dynamic imaging. This means it can capture dozens of 3D images consecutively within one second, akin to a "molecular-level high-speed camera." It expands PET applications from glucose metabolism to oxygen and lipid metabolism, and advances from static imaging to observing dynamic processes such as neural stimulation, blood flow perfusion, and drug metabolism. This not only provides new methods for diagnosing and assisting in treating diseases of systemic systems like blood and immunity but also reveals life science phenomena such as the interaction mechanisms within complex systems like metabolism and the tumor microenvironment for various diseases.

Professor Yaming Li, Honorary Chairman of the Nuclear Medicine Branch of the Chinese Medical Association, Chairman of the Nuclear Medicine Branch of the Chinese Nuclear Society, and professor at China Medical University, believes this technology evolves PET from an "anatomical-metabolic camera" to a "live broadcast camera for life processes," potentially revolutionizing treatment decisions guided by medical imaging while providing a new observational dimension for understanding the essence of life.

Since the beginning of this year, the industrialization process of digital PET has entered a fast track. Besides the DigitMI 9120, Raysolution’s DigitMI 960 with a 65-cm axial length has also received certification and entered the market. To date, the company has brought to market: 7 models of clinical whole-body all-digital PET/CT devices, 1 brain-dedicated all-digital PET, and 1 CT device. By creating a multi-model, multi-functional product matrix covering different clinical needs, the company has become one of the domestic PET equipment manufacturers with the most extensive product lines in the Chinese market.

It is worth noting that according to recent statistics published by the Nuclear Medicine Physician Branch of the Chinese Medical Doctor Association, Raysolution appeared for the first time on the list of the most influential brands for positron emission tomography equipment in the first quarter of 2025, ranking second among domestic brands in terms of domestic market share.

Dr. Dandan Zhu, Director of the Department of Nuclear Medicine at the Hefei Cancer Hospital of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, believes that as domestic high-end medical devices have progressively achieved independent control over core technologies, breaking through technical barriers will inevitably further break down the market barriers of imported products. Additionally, with the significant reduction in procurement costs and maintenance thresholds for domestic equipment, the process of domestic substitution in the high-end medical device field is now unstoppable.
