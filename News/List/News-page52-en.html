Xinhua News Agency, Lueersheim, Germany, March 17: Exclusive Interview: I Became a 'Living Advertisement' for Advanced Research in China - An Interview with German Graduate Student Niklas Miller

By Zhang Yirong, Xinhua News Agency

“I have become the school’s ‘living advertisement’. Both the university and I hope that more students will choose to come to China to participate in cutting-edge international scientific research,” said German master's student Niklas Miller with a smile when interviewed by Xinhua.

Recently, an 'endorsement letter' written by Niklas Miller, a master’s student of Applied Physics at RheinMain University of Applied Sciences in Germany, was featured on the university’s homepage. In his article, he introduced his three-month exchange internship experience at Huazhong University of Science and Technology's Digital PET (Positron Emission Tomography) Laboratory starting from September last year, and strongly recommended other students to apply for similar opportunities. The article also includes photos taken by him in places like the Great Wall of China.

Miller wrote that through this internship, “I have learned a lot both professionally and personally, and it has given me new perspectives on my future life and career development.”

He told reporters that he is currently preparing his master’s thesis titled 'Development of Blood Oxygen Saturation Monitoring Ring Using Fully Digital Silicon Photomultiplier Tubes'. In simple terms, the project aims to provide a technical solution for developing a ring capable of measuring blood oxygen levels. This is precisely the research project I participated in at Huazhong University of Science and Technology's Digital PET Laboratory.

Miller explained that this high-tech multi-functional ring will have significant medical clinical application value, including early diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease. “The relevant scientific research technology by the Chinese team is among the world’s top. This was also a major reason why I signed up for the program,” he said.

Looking back on his internship experience in China, Miller expressed amazement: 'Although it wasn’t long, the intensity was enormous. The dedication and hard work of my Chinese mentors and research partners left an extremely deep impression on me. It felt like they were fully committed to their research. Being part of this environment, I couldn't help but be deeply influenced.'

Walking through the teaching buildings at RheinMain University of Applied Sciences, Miller told reporters that unlike in Germany, professors’ offices are almost always open in China. “You can go seek help for any problem and will always receive warm and enthusiastic guidance,” he said. “Even now back in Germany, I still maintain contact with my Chinese mentors through WeChat.”

Apart from his studies, Miller also made time to travel around China. “After the internship ended last December, I spent a month traveling to Xi’an, Beijing, Shanghai, Hong Kong, Hainan... Most of the time, I traveled by high-speed rail. Throughout my journey, you could always feel the vastness and rapid development of China,” Miller said.

When talking about his future plans, Miller told reporters that he is considering further studies in digital PET technology. “Last year, Huazhong University of Science and Technology’s Digital PET Laboratory collaborated with a brain research center in Italy to establish an overseas research center there. I hope to continue staying within the research team and participate in projects across China, Italy, and Germany in the future, aiming for a doctoral degree,” he said.

Miller added: “To work and live better in the future, I am also preparing to learn Chinese. Learning some Chinese is my advice for students who plan to come to China for exchange internships.” (End)
