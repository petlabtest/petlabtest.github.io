In the Laboratory's vocabulary, 'light' has never been merely a noun. It is the 511 keV photon born when a positron meets an electron, the burst of visible scintillation in a crystal, the electrical pulse on a photodetector, and a precise point on an image. What links all of these together are people: those who believe in light, pursue light, and eventually become light themselves. This article tells the story of the Laboratory's mission, research, and educational philosophy, and of the first beam of light it prepares for young people: the Undergraduate Practice Opportunities Program.

Where the Light Comes From: A Laboratory's Mission

In 2004, the Digital PET Laboratory was founded at Huazhong University of Science and Technology. From its first day, its mission pointed toward a larger question: to unravel the origins of the universe, life, and consciousness, advancing scientific progress through bold intellectual endeavor and safeguarding human well-being.

The mission sounds vast, but the Laboratory has brought it down to a specific coordinate: pioneering the uncharted. Positron emission tomography (PET) is one of the most important molecular imaging tools for observing the metabolic activity of life, yet the analog signal processing of conventional PET is approaching its limits in time resolution, quantitative accuracy, and information fidelity. The Laboratory chose to reinvent PET from the bottom up, so that every annihilation event is recorded digitally at its source, turning 'the most invisible light' into 'the clearest images.'

The Laboratory's development has always kept pace with national strategic priorities, key industrial bottlenecks, and major public health challenges. A full-chain innovation system covering basic research, technological innovation, clinical translation, and industrial application has been its unchanged roadmap for more than two decades.

The Work of Chasing Light: From Photons to the Picture of Life

The Laboratory's research can be summarized in one sentence: starting from photons, gaining a clearer picture of life. Along this main line, the Laboratory has laid out core technologies including high-sensitivity detection, ultrahigh temporal resolution, and dynamic quantitative imaging.

On the detection side, the Laboratory's scintillation crystals and photodetectors convert 511 keV annihilation photons step by step into visible light, electrical pulses, and data; under the multi-voltage threshold (MVT) sampling method, the time, energy, and position of every annihilation event are precisely digitized at the source, and the raw information is preserved, traceable, and reprocessable. This is the physical foundation of all-digital PET and the confidence behind the Laboratory's claim of 'reinventing PET.'

On the application side, all-digital PET is entering more and more scenarios: tumor metabolic imaging, brain and cardiac function assessment, drug development, plant physiology, and the Laboratory's key initiative of online proton-therapy monitoring. Innovative systems such as Digital PET 2.0 turn 'from photons to the picture of life' from a slogan into real operating instruments.

Passing the Light On: How the Laboratory Develops People

The Laboratory's educational philosophy comes from more than two decades of teaching by example: solid technical competence is a baseline qualification, while what truly makes a researcher stand out is the ability to explain complex ideas clearly and to collaborate effectively. Mentors repeatedly emphasize that clear expression and professional communication are as critical as technical skills.

The Laboratory brings together researchers in hardware, software, and applications, and cross-disciplinary collaboration is the norm: people who grow crystals, people who build circuits, people who write algorithms, and people who work with clinicians meet at the same instrument. Openness, collaboration, mutual benefit, and integrity are the principles the Laboratory shares with its partners, and the earliest lessons its young members learn.

For young people, the Laboratory believes one thing: doing is the fastest way to understand the world. From assembling detector rings to debugging imaging algorithms, from designing circuits to writing reports, every step is open to students.

UPOP: The First Beam of Light for Young People

The Undergraduate Practice Opportunities Program (UPOP) is a co-curricular professional development program exclusively for sophomores at the Digital PET Laboratory, and the first beam of light the Laboratory offers to young believers in light. Its goal is simple and clear: to help students find, secure, and thrive in their first professional experience, whether in industry, research, or academia.

UPOP offers a complete growth path:

  • One-on-one advising: regular meetings with UPOP staff to plan career paths;
  • Seven milestone workshops: starting from resumes, cover letters, and professional connections, advancing to project management, professional communication, and personal skills;
  • Team Training Workshops (TTW): multi-day experiential learning focused on teamwork, communication, and problem solving;
  • The UPOP mentor network: mentors from various fields share their career experience;
  • The UPOP employer network: exclusive internship and career opportunities for UPOP students;
  • Lifetime access to UPOP resources: program resources remain open to alumni.

By the end of UPOP, students are able to craft personalized resumes and persuasive cover letters, present themselves confidently in interviews and social settings, execute a targeted summer experience, lead structured projects in teams, and translate teamwork into problem-solving ability.

Student testimonials confirm the value of this path: some gained a complete map of abilities, 'from negotiation skills to the unwritten rules of the workplace'; some learned to express themselves better in one-on-one mentor meetings; others called UPOP 'an extraordinary opportunity that many of us rarely get in our careers.'

The UPOP year consists of three separate courses: EPE fall semester (with four milestone workshops), EPW (including the Team Training Workshop), and EPE spring semester (with three milestone workshops); once registered, the Laboratory provides all course information. For any questions, students may contact petlab@hust.edu.cn at any time, and the UPOP team is glad to help; the office in Room 401 is always open for a visit, for snacks, coffee, or tea, or to reserve a room for interviews and meetings.

The Laboratory looks forward to exploring, inventing, innovating, and creating together with partners around the world. Whether you apply to UPOP, join a research team, or are simply curious about light, the Laboratory's door is open. That beam of light will not disappoint those who believe in it.
