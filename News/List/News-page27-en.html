Domestic "Cancer Warning Aircraft" Achieves Difficult Innovation from 0 to 1

On April 6, the Economic Daily reporter learned from Huazhong University of Science and Technology that the world's first clinical all-digital PET/CT invented by the university's Xie Qingguo team was put into trial operation in Ezhou Central Hospital in Hubei Province in June 2020...
2021-06-23
Economic Daily

On April 6, the Economic Daily reported that Huazhong University of Science and Technology announced that the world's first clinical full-digital PET/CT, invented by Professor Xie Qingguo's team, was put into trial operation at Ezhou Central Hospital in Hubei Province in June 2020. In the past ten months, this 'sharp-eyed' 'cancer early warning machine' has detected cancer lesions for hundreds of patients ahead of time, completing the arduous process from research results to clinical application and achieving a leapfrog development from zero to one in domestic innovative medical devices.

Professor Xie Qingguo's team spent twenty years developing the world's first full-digital PET. After entering clinical 'battle', it has proven the feasibility of this new technological route with concrete clinical cases, as well as the enormous potential of this disruptive innovation technology.

Detect cancer lesions ahead of time

In May 2020, Ms. Wang, a 29-year-old nurse at Ezhou Central Hospital, was found to have lung nodules during her routine physical examination. As she had no discomfort symptoms initially, she did not pay much attention. Coincidentally, the clinical full-digital PET/CT equipment began trial operation in June when it was installed. Thinking that there would be no need to queue for a PET scan like other hospitals, Ms. Wang followed the doctor's advice and underwent a PET examination. Upon checking, she was shocked: the digital PET imaging showed an irregular white shadow on her right lung, slightly brighter than surrounding tissues.

Jiao Cili, head of nuclear medicine at Ezhou Central Hospital, explained that if there is a tumor in the human body, it will show hypermetabolic activity; malignant tumors are more metabolically active than benign ones. After injecting a certain amount of tracer into the patient's body, the imaging would display brighter areas indicating tumor lesions compared to surrounding tissues.

"Due to its ultra-high biochemical sensitivity, digital PET can detect tumor lesions with relatively low metabolic levels and less severe conditions. Although the brightness of the white shadow in Ms. Wang’s lungs was not high, it clearly indicated that cancer had occurred there." Jiao Cili immediately advised Ms. Wang to undergo targeted treatment promptly. Following Dr. Jiao's advice, Ms. Wang underwent timely surgical resection, effectively preventing further deterioration and spread of the lesion.

Jiao Cili introduced that since its trial operation last June, this equipment has been in use for ten months. Initially, it received one citizen every four days on average. As word-of-mouth grew, now there are five people per day undergoing PET/CT examinations. Many citizens who previously traveled between Ezhou and Wuhan for regular follow-ups have come to Ezhou Central Hospital for appointments. 'Previously, Ezhou residents had to go to other cities or even provinces for PET scans; now not only local residents but also those from nearby cities specifically book appointments at our hospital. Today alone we received two patients from Huangshi for checkups and a few days ago several citizens from Huanggang made reservations,' Jiao Cili said.

Zhang Bo, the industrialization leader of clinical full-digital PET, introduced that despite limited team resources, this product is currently in its first generation version with basic hardware configuration but has already demonstrated significant potential. It is expected to launch a second-generation device by year-end, which will reduce a whole-body PET scan time to 3.5 minutes.

Jiao Cili believes that compared to traditional PET, the most distinctive feature of digital PET equipment is its 'usability'. 'Because it has achieved digitization and modularity, maintenance is particularly easy. If there's a problem with any part, you just need to replace one module without affecting other parts like in traditional PET maintenance, which often requires a lot of time and effort.'

For patients, the most direct experience is quick and accurate examination. If the examinee accidentally moves during the scan causing image artifacts or sudden interruptions, digital PET does not require re-scanning but can correct it through image processing software. It achieves fast and accurate results while reducing radiation dose.

Digital PET's breakthrough from 0 to 1

PET research has extremely high barriers, previously only a few Western countries had mastered PET development technology. The digitization of PET is hailed as the 'Two Bombs and One Satellite' in the medical equipment industry. Professor Xie Qingguo’s team solved the international challenge of digitalizing PET signal sources with their MVT method, achieving originality and leadership in global digital PET research.

The National Health Commission's PET/CT configuration plan released in 2018 showed that by the end of 2020, there would be a total of 710 PET/CT units nationwide. Statistics show that in 2016, the United States had 5.13 units per million population, Australia had 3.07 units, while China had less than 0.2 units per million population. China has the world's largest number of cancer patients, with an average of seven people diagnosed with cancer every minute. According to a World Health Organization survey, one-third of cancers can be completely cured if detected early.

Xiao Peng, a key member of the digital PET team and professor at Huazhong University of Science and Technology, introduced that 'as a powerful tool for early diagnosis of cancer, the PET market has huge potential. Currently, the number of installed PET units in China is far from meeting public health needs; it's mainly provided by tertiary hospitals in first- and second-tier cities.'

Liu Liming, Secretary of the Party Committee and President of Ezhou Central Hospital, believes that as the smallest prefecture-level city in Hubei Province, Ezhou can welcome the installation of the world’s first clinical full-digital PET equipment locally. This 'small' city welcoming a 'big' device will significantly enhance Ezhou's tumor detection technology level and strengthen its medical resources.

Currently, we have completed the research and application breakthrough from 0 to 1 for digital PET in Ezhou. Next, digital PET needs to quickly achieve large-scale, high-quality applications and enter the fast lane of development from 1 to N. We also look forward to more local governments like Ezhou joining us to promote this 'fast lane' for digital PET as a major national health instrument that benefits both the country and its people.' The inventor of digital PET, Professor Xie Qingguo from Huazhong University of Science and Technology, expressed his expectations. (Written by Liu Jie, Economic Daily Jingdian Science Studio)
