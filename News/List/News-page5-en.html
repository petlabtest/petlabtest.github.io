New medical imaging equipment frequently leaves the laboratory in Wuhan

In July, the world's first musculoskeletal ultrasound system will enter clinical use, while a breast ultrasound system that has just completed clinical trials will also be launched soon...
2024-07-10
Yangtze River Daily

Changjiang Daily, May 27, 2024

Contributor Wang Xiaoxiao Yang Ya

In July this year, the world's first B-ultrasound machine for musculoskeletal use will enter clinical practice. A breast-specific B-ultrasound that has just completed clinical trials is also about to be launched. Last year alone, five high-end medical imaging devices of the PET category, including all-digital PET/CT (Positron Emission Tomography), a brain-specialized all-digital PET, and a proton therapy navigation all-digital PET, entered the market. Meanwhile, cranial B-ultrasound is under development, and an upgraded micron-level MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging) system is in the works... Changjiang Daily reporters discovered that high-end medical imaging equipment has been achieving significant breakthroughs in Wuhan, with more affordable, clearer, and more precise devices continuously entering the market from here. These medical imaging devices developed in Wuhan not only provide patients and doctors with more options but also accelerate the import substitution in the field of medical imaging equipment.

■ Can See Bones and Vessels Like a CT

New B-ultrasound to Enter Clinical Use in July

"Can see bones and vessels!" On May 14 at noon, in Guoptu's Visual Medical Imaging Co., Ltd. (hereinafter referred to as Visual Medical), the scene was bustling with activity. The research team was making final preparations for this new B-ultrasound before clinical use. Reporters witnessed the magical imaging capabilities of this new B-ultrasound on-site.

When I put my right hand into a 'water tank', after 30 seconds, a three-dimensional image of the bones, vessels, and nerves in my right hand appeared on the computer screen, revealing all internal structures clearly.

"The effect of the new B-ultrasound is comparable to that of CT," Professor Ding Mingyue, chief scientist at Visual Medical, pointed to a one-and-a-half-meter-square 'box' beside him. The new B-ultrasound consists of three parts: control console, scanning device, and bed frame. The control console mainly handles signal acquisition and image reconstruction. The 'water tank' is the signal acquisition device, filled with water as an acoustic coupling agent. Inside the 'water tank', there is a key component of the new B-ultrasound—the 'ring-shaped ultrasound probe,' which can collect sound wave signals from 360 degrees, ensuring that the water acts as a coupling agent to ensure the collection of sound waves.

"The key reason why the new B-ultrasound is more powerful than traditional ones lies in its superior signal processing capabilities," Professor Ding Mingyue explained further. Traditional B-ultrasounds have only 64 or 128 signal receivers, whereas the new one has 2048 signal receivers, all concentrated on the 'ring-shaped ultrasound probe,' enabling full scatter signal collection from 360 degrees, including transmission, reflection, diffusion, and diffraction. Even with 128 signal receivers in mainstream traditional B-ultrasounds, only 6% of photon signals can be received; however, the new one can achieve nearly 100% reception of sound wave signals through the 'ring-shaped ultrasound probe,' then reconstruct cross-sectional images based on these signals, and the 'ring-shaped ultrasound probe' is crucial for image clarity.

Professor Ding Mingyue said that in July this year, a new B-ultrasound named 'Musculoskeletal Ultrasound Tomography System' will officially enter hospital clinical use. Compared to traditional B-ultrasounds, the new one is larger and its probes can be made into various shapes such as ring-shaped, semi-ring-shaped, or spherical probes for different purposes.

"The new B-ultrasound is essentially an ultrasound CT," Professor Ding Mingyue explained. CT is actually a technology rather than a product itself, belonging to computerized tomography imaging technology. "Compared with CT, the greatest advantage of the new B-ultrasound for patients is safety." Professor Zheng Yuanyi, vice president of Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital, who participated in clinical trials of the new B-ultrasound, said that traditional B-ultrasounds can only see limb cross-sections, whereas the new one can visualize the entire musculoskeletal system, which is more beneficial for diagnosis.

In a corner of Visual Medical’s R&D department, a ‘cranial ultrasound tomography’ machine taller than a person was undergoing final technical breakthroughs. The company's chief technology officer, Professor Wei Chiming, put on a helmet equipped with a 'circular ultrasonic probe', and took his first picture of the brain.

"The cranial images are somewhat blurry and we still have some distance to go, but we are confident," Professor Wei Chiming told reporters. Previously, it was believed that ultrasound could not be used for adult skull examinations because the signals would be reflected back by the skull bone without entering the brain.

“It won’t be long before we can use new B-ultrasound technology for cranial examinations on patients,” said Professor Ding Mingyue. After ten years of effort, they have overcome technical challenges in musculoskeletal and breast ultrasound imaging; achieving breakthroughs in cranial ultrasound is just a matter of time.

Last year, the team led by Professor Ding Mingyue completed clinical trials for their breast ultrasound tomography system, which was included in Hubei Province's second-class innovative medical device special review procedure and will soon obtain a medical device registration certificate. The product can enter the market this year.

■ Full Body Scan Takes Only 80 Seconds

All-Digital PET Achieves Overseas Sales

While Professor Ding Mingyue’s team was making breakthroughs in ultrasound CT, another team at Rui Shi Medical, located 40 kilometers away, achieved remarkable results in the PET field. The DigitMI 930, an all-digital PET/CT developed by Rui Shi Medical, received national medical device registration certification from the National Medical Products Administration in 2022 and officially entered the market. This equipment can complete a single bed scan in just 20 seconds, with a full body scan taking only 80 seconds, making it the fastest single-bed imaging speed globally.

On May 10th morning, at Rui Shi Medical’s standard production workshop in Wuhan New City, a newly produced all-digital PET/CT device was immediately taken away by customers. 'We can assemble all-digital PET like Lego toys,' said Dr. Li Bingxuan, product director of Rui Shi Medical. 'For example, we have brain-specialized all-digital PET and proton therapy navigation all-digital PET.' Last year alone, five products were approved for market entry.

Dr. Li Bingxuan graduated from Huazhong University of Science and Technology’s Biomedical Engineering program in 2013 and is a key member of Professor Xie Qingguo's team, focusing on full digital PET imaging methods and medical applications research for over fifteen years. He was also one of the main drafters of China's Digital PET standards.

Dr. Li Bingxuan introduced that the principle of PET imaging involves injecting radioactive isotopes labeled with fluorine-18 glucose into a patient’s vein, which then releases gamma photons through a series of reactions. The PET detects these photons to locate malignant tumors and other lesions.

How to accurately collect three pieces of information about photons (time, position, energy) using detectors is the key to developing PET.

Professor Xie Qingguo proposed a unique multi-voltage threshold sampling (MVT) method that can achieve far superior results than traditional PET at extremely low cost and power consumption. 'The full digitalization of PET is not just an upgrade in technology; it's reinventing PET,' said Professor Xie. This innovation could bring revolutionary changes, akin to the transition from film cameras to digital ones.

PET is a crucial device for precision medicine, primarily used for diagnosing tumors and cardiovascular diseases alongside CT and MRI as medical imaging 'big three'. Dr. Lan Xiaoli, director of Huazhong University of Science and Technology Union Hospital’s PET Center, told reporters that domestically produced PETs are technologically on par with imported ones, with many parameters even better than foreign products.

"Compared to similar overseas products, all-digital PET leads in key performance metrics such as resolution and sensitivity," Professor Xie Qingguo's team won the World Intellectual Property Organization Global Award in 2022 for their original MVT method.

■ Let Early Diseases Nowhere to Hide

Micron-Level MRI System Will Debut

In 2005, at age 44, Professor Ding Mingyue returned from overseas research on three-dimensional ultrasound to Huazhong University of Science and Technology. In 2007, at the age of 32, Professor Wei Chiming also returned from studying abroad to join Huazhong University of Science and Technology. They both switched from automatic control to biomedical engineering and began focusing on ultrasound research. Professor Ding Mingyue recalled that Western countries were leading in ultrasonic tomography development, constantly trying 'ultrasound + CT' combinations. In 2013, the first US-made ultrasonic tomography system entered the market at $700,000 per unit.

In 2005 as well, Professor Xie Qingguo switched from automatic control to biomedical engineering and began teaching at Huazhong University of Science and Technology. He established a digital PET laboratory focusing on positron emission tomography (PET) method research and instrument development before becoming colleagues with Ding Mingyue and Wei Chiming. At that time, everyone was determined to compete with imported PETs.

In 1980, under the approval of the Ministry of Education, Huazhong Institute of Technology (the predecessor of Huazhong University of Science and Technology) established a bioengineering program in its mechanics department, which later led to the creation of the Bioengineering Department. In 1982, Professor Wang Junjian from the Bioengineering Department, Professor Kang Huaguang from the Information Engineering Department, and Professor Xu Huibei from the Chemistry Department jointly applied for the establishment of China's first biomedical engineering doctoral program at Huazhong Institute of Technology, pioneering interdisciplinary integration in the field of biomedical engineering.

"Conduct interdisciplinary research in response to national needs." Professor Wang Junjian, who is over ninety years old, recalls the original intention behind founding the biomedical engineering program and expresses great satisfaction with today's achievements.

In recent years, with the arrival of the big data era, the field of bioinformatics has developed rapidly. Biomedical engineering technology has achieved a 'one-stop' development from information collection to data integration and analysis, providing strong data support for precision medicine and personalized treatment.

It is reported that currently, the localization rate of high-end medical equipment in our country is less than 30%. In the past, international brands held 90% of the Chinese market share for PET, which has now dropped to 60%, with domestic PETs represented by Rui Shi Medical reshaping the domestic market landscape.

Professor Wang Weidong, the group leader of the Ministry of Science and Technology's Key Research and Development Program expert panel, stated that the medical imaging equipment research team led by all-digital PET from Huazhong University has achieved independent innovation over 20 years. All core components have been localized, not only promoting the development of precision medicine in China but also driving the entire industry chain. Wuhan is now an important source of origin for domestic medical imaging equipment R&D.

In the MRI field, one of the 'three major pieces' in medical imaging, Huazhong University's team has its own plans.

"Within five years, after optimization and upgrading, the pulsed high magnetic field experimental device under Luojia Mountain will reach 110 teslas, and the performance of MRI will also be significantly improved." Professor Li Liang, director of the National Pulsed High Magnetic Field Center, said that in magnetic resonance imaging, the current 3 tesla or 1.5 tesla can be increased to 9.5 tesla, improving sub-millimeter imaging to 30 micrometers, enhancing by one order of magnitude, which is more effective for early diagnosis of diseases and will launch a new upgraded version of micron-level magnetic resonance imaging systems (MRI).

"The performance of MRI (image resolution) is positively correlated with the strength of the magnetic field; an increase in strength leads to improved performance." Professor Cao Quanliang from the National Pulsed High Magnetic Field Center stated that MRI consists of a magnet, spectroscopy, and data processing systems, with the magnet being the core of R&D. Once this core issue is resolved, everything else will fall into place.
