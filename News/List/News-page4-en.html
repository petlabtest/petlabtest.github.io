Digital PET Device Capable of “Visualizing” Proton Beams Now Available!

The innovative Digital PET 2.0 system - a technology uniquely developed by our team - has achieved significant breakthroughs!
March 8, 2025
Digital PET Laboratory

The innovative Digital PET 2.0 system - a technology uniquely developed by our team - has achieved significant breakthroughs!

Recently, the proton beam energy transport observation device for biological tissues (referred to as the device), developed under the National Major Scientific Instrument Project, has been successfully developed. After installation at Hefei Ion Medical Center and Wuhan Union Hospital Proton Medicine Center, the device underwent four years of equipment development and preclinical testing, successfully achieving submillimeter online monitoring of the three-dimensional dynamic distribution of proton beam-induced positron nuclides and proton beam range. This is expected to provide clearer "visualization" of proton beam irradiation breadth and depth, thereby enabling more precise proton therapy for tumors.

The successfully developed proton beam observation device consists of two main components: a proton beam-induced secondary particle detection system and a data processing workstation.

According to Qiuhui Ma, a Digital PET researcher and PhD candidate at Huazhong University of Science and Technology's Digital PET Laboratory, the device's standout technical feature is its high counting rate.

Leveraging the signal source digitization advantage of the Multi-Voltage Threshold (MVT) method, the device's maximum counting rate exceeds 48 Mcps, enabling more comprehensive capture of positron annihilation gamma signals induced by proton beams. Capturing these signals equates to tracking the proton beam's path through tissue, providing essential prerequisites for in-beam monitoring of proton therapy.

In 2018, the digital PET team developed the world's first dual-panel digital PET system for proton therapy monitoring. The newly developed proton beam observation device, which entered R&D in January 2020, was preliminarily completed in December 2024 after over four years of development.

Compared to the first-generation prototype, the new device features comprehensive upgrades and innovations. In hardware systems, the detection system's effective detection area has been expanded to 3.5 times the previous size, achieving higher counting rates, improved sensitivity, and enhanced spatial and temporal resolution.

On the software front, addressing the need for rapid processing of proton therapy monitoring signals, Professor Lin Wan's software team upgraded the Plug-n-Image software platform. This platform implements GPU-based pulse reconstruction, event coincidence, and image reconstruction functions, achieving "one-click imaging" from data acquisition to reconstructed images.

Since undertaking project tasks in 2020, Digital PET researchers have received strong support from relevant institutions in Wuhan and Hefei. Both Wuhan Union Hospital Proton Medicine Center and Hefei Ion Medical Center invested significant resources, coordinating valuable experimental space and conditions. The digital PET team conducted multiple discussions with Dr. Sheng Zhang's team and Physicist Zhiyong Yang's team at Union Hospital Proton Medicine Center, as well as Dr. Shuanghu Yuan's team and Physicist Xiaoming Lu's team at Hefei Ion Medical Center, regarding proton beam monitoring experiments and jointly developed necessary treatment plans.

The device has currently undergone a series of equipment debugging and preclinical tests, conducting experiments including proton beam irradiation of water phantoms, PMMA phantoms, head model phantoms, pork, and animal subjects.

These experiments successfully achieved submillimeter online monitoring of the three-dimensional dynamic distribution of proton beam-induced positron nuclides and proton beam range, while exploring glucose metabolic changes during proton therapy and analyzing proton beam biological washout effects in organisms. Breakthroughs in related technologies are expected to play crucial roles in dose guidance, biological guidance, treatment evaluation, and equipment quality control for proton radiotherapy.

Proton therapy, known as a "powerful cancer treatment tool," represents internationally advanced cancer treatment technology. According to relevant statistics, since the first clinical application of proton therapy in 1954, by the end of 2021, the global number of proton therapy patients reached 280,000, accounting for 86.2% of all particle therapy patients. This number is projected to increase to 350,000 by the end of 2025, reflecting the rapid adoption and growing clinical recognition of proton therapy technology.
