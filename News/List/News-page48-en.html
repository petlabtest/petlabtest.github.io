Sensors Publishes Xie Qingguo Team’s Research on Precise Online Monitoring of Proton Therapy

2018-09-30
Guangming Daily

(September 30, 2018 17:53 Guangming Daily Client ZHAO Jing) On September 8, 2018, the authoritative international journal Sensors published the latest research results of Professor XIE Qingguo's team from Huazhong University of Science and Technology in China. The title of the paper is 'A Plug-and-Play PET System for Proton Therapy Monitoring'. This study verified the potential and feasibility of digital PET technology applied to online monitoring during proton therapy, enabling precise knowledge of the actual range and delivered dose of proton beams after they enter human tissues. It solves a world-class challenge in real-time monitoring of proton beams, thereby effectively enhancing the efficacy of proton knife treatment for tumors, improving patients' cure rates and survival rates.

Proton radiotherapy is one of the most advanced cancer radiotherapy techniques, often referred to as 'a cancer-fighting weapon'. Compared with traditional therapies, proton therapy overcomes the dilemma of conventional radiation and chemotherapy where 'killing a thousand enemies means harming eight hundred friends', utilizing the characteristic of protons' Bragg peak to release energy at specific tissue depths. This achieves precise targeting of tumors for 'stereotactic blasting'. Due to its strong penetration ability, good dose distribution, and high local dose characteristics, proton therapy shows significant advantages in treating tumors surrounded by important organs. At the same time, it is renowned as a 'sky-high priced surgery', with domestic proton radiotherapy costing around 200,000 yuan per session, while American proton radiotherapy fees range from $110,000 to $140,000.

A fully digital flat-panel PET system in the proton therapy room

The actual range and delivered dose of proton beams within the human body require precise monitoring methods. Since proton beams entering the human body produce positron nuclides that subsequently annihilate to generate gamma photons, PET technology can detect these gamma photons to reverse engineer the position and quantity of positrons, thereby obtaining information on the delivered dose and actual range of proton beams. Therefore, PET is considered the most promising method for monitoring proton therapy. Since the 1990s, global scientists have been exploring the feasibility of using PET for proton beam monitoring. However, due to its 'analog-digital hybrid' characteristics, traditional PET cannot provide real-time and precise monitoring of proton beams entering the human body.

One of the co-authors of the paper, Vice President Hong Zhihong of Linkou Changgung Memorial Hospital, introduced that if proton therapy cannot achieve absolute precision in targeting tumor lesions, it often leads to incomplete treatment, recurrence of tumors, or side effects. Specifically, proton therapy with monitoring systems transforms 'theoretical perfection' into 'practical perfection', truly maximizing the effectiveness of proton therapy. This is precisely the core issue facing clinical applications of proton radiotherapy.

Since 2016, Professor XIE Qingguo's team has conducted multiple experimental studies at Linkou Changgung Memorial Hospital. Last November, scientists from both sides used a fully digital flat-panel PET for online monitoring of proton beams in Linkou Changgung Memorial Hospital and made significant discoveries. The inventor of digital PET and professor at Huazhong University of Science and Technology, XIE Qingguo, said that during the experiment, they first monitored oxygen-15 produced when proton beams hit human tissues using a flat-panel fully digital PET system, indicating that digital PET is highly likely to achieve precise monitoring of positron emission distribution resulting from beta-plus decay of protons, thereby tracking the delivered dose and actual range of proton beams.

Nicola Dascenzo, first author of the paper and core member of the team, a full-time professor at Huazhong University of Science and Technology, stated that oxygen-15 has a half-life of only 2 minutes. Therefore, using PET for online monitoring of oxygen-15 during proton beam therapy imposes extremely stringent requirements on system spatial resolution, time resolution, etc. Nicola said: 'This experiment fully verified the outstanding performance of digital PET. Using digital PET can collect large amounts of raw data when protons enter living bodies, thereby monitoring the trajectory and action process of proton beams within the human body, a feat unprecedented globally. This means that the challenge of online monitoring with proton knives may be solved, potentially greatly improving the treatment effect of protons on cancer.'

According to data from the International Particle Therapy Cooperative Group (PTCOG) website (as of June 2017): Currently, there are 62 operational proton centers worldwide, and it is expected that by 2020, a total of 131 particle therapy centers will be in operation globally. China currently has three operational proton centers. Hong Zhihong introduced that according to data released by the World Health Organization's International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), there are approximately 14 million new cancer cases annually worldwide, with conservative estimates suggesting that one million cases meet the criteria for proton radiotherapy. Even with rapid development, it still falls far short of meeting medical needs.

XIE Qingguo introduced that current digital PET mainly focuses on methods to achieve full digital imaging. 'Although the prospects are broad, significant challenges remain in further integrating research between proton therapy and digital PET at this stage.' XIE Qingguo said, first, such research requires comprehensive infrastructure support and substantial financial investment. Second, proton therapy and digital PET belong to two different fields; resources need to be integrated from equipment, personnel, technology, projects, etc., on all fronts. Third, digital PET's joint research with proton therapy has entered a scientific 'no man’s land' globally without any precedents to follow. 'I hope that relevant national departments can quickly plan for technological breakthroughs in precise proton radiotherapy and coordinate related research resources to seize the strategic opportunity of China's digital PET in this field, contributing to humanity's early conquest of cancer treatment challenges.'
