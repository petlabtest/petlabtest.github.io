Xie Qingguo: Building Digital PET into Optics Valley Business Card (Text/Liao Wuzhou)

Name card: Xie Qingguo, male, born in 1972, from Xiaoxita Town, Yichang, Hubei...
2015-08-06
Digital PET Laboratory

Profile: Xie Qingguo, male, born in 1972 in Xiaoxitata Town, Yichang City, Hubei Province. In 1990, he was admitted to Huazhong University of Science and Technology and obtained his doctoral degree from the same university. Since 2001, he and his team have been dedicated to research on positron emission tomography (PET) instruments. He pioneered the MVT method and worked with his team to solve the world's challenging problem of digitizing high-speed scintillation pulses at their source. In 2010, his team developed the world's first fully digital PET, holding all intellectual property rights. In October 2013, this research achievement won the First Prize for Technological Invention in Hubei Province. In May 2013, Xie Qingguo was selected into the 'Optics Valley 3551 Talent Program'.
 
       Starting from a laboratory converted from a toilet, Professor Xie Qingguo and his team, with their indomitable spirit, overcame numerous technological challenges in extremely rudimentary conditions for more than ten years to develop PET technology that is at the forefront of the world.
       This international research team spanning over ten disciplines pioneered the MVT method in 2003 to solve the global challenge of high-speed scintillation pulse digitalization in PET, and completed the world's first fully digital PET in 2010. In 2013, under Mayor Tang Liangzhi's directive, the industrialization layout for clinical full-digital PET was established in Wuhan, with plans to locate a Digital PET Application Demonstration Center in the Optics Valley Biocity Medical Device Industrial Park. Leveraging Wuhan’s application-oriented advantages, the Digital PET is expected to become an 'Optics Valley brand' and even a 'Wuhan brand', centered around the Application Demonstration Center.
 
      Starting from the high ground of Wuhan
      PET is one of the most advanced medical imaging technologies in today's world following X-ray machines, CT scans, and MRI. Its main characteristic is its sensitivity to biochemical reactions, enabling it to image metabolic activities within human cells. Since any disease precursor involves biochemistry, PET has an irreplaceable advantage in early diagnosis and efficacy evaluation for major diseases, especially cancer, heart disease, brain, and neurological disorders. Particularly for cancer, PET can very sensitively detect tiny early-stage tumors, increasing the possibility of cure. Moreover, PET is known as the 'gold standard' for assessing myocardial viability clinically and can predict the likelihood of Alzheimer's disease (senile dementia) before its onset.
      Academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering and the founder of PET applications in Central China, Fan Mingwu once said: “Initially promoting the PET application industry was due to recognizing its immeasurable value to society, as it has irreplaceable advantages in diagnosing major diseases.” After years of accumulation, Wuhan has developed into a hub for this technology, with currently five operational PET centers.
      Xie Qingguo graduated from Huazhong University of Science and Technology (HUST) with a bachelor's degree and obtained his doctorate there in 2001. Upon graduation, Xie began to assemble the digital PET team and pioneered the MVT (multi-voltage threshold) method in 2003, solving the world-renowned challenge of digitizing high-speed scintillation pulses from their source for PET.
      The principle behind PET imaging is extraordinarily complex, with extremely high barriers for core instrument technology development. It involves over ten disciplines such as biomedical engineering, telecommunications, electronics, control systems, computer science, mathematics, mechanics, nuclear medicine, radiology, and clinical medicine. To this end, his laboratory has fully leveraged the cross-disciplinary advantages of HUST in medical engineering. Over a decade, they have gathered a team of more than 50 researchers from over ten disciplines including biomedical engineering, telecommunications, electronics, control systems, computer science, mathematics, mechanics, nuclear medicine, radiology, and clinical medicine. During this period, they received total project funding exceeding one hundred million yuan, obtained over thirty international patents in China, the United States, Germany, Japan, among others, and published more than sixty papers in authoritative international journals or conferences such as IEEE TNS.
 
      Reaching out to the forefront of the world again
      As a core leader of the team, Xie Qingguo traveled extensively for further studies after dedicating himself to PET as his lifelong career. From 2002 to 2008, he studied in Taiwan and the United States. This experience not only laid the foundation for Xie's work on PET but also broadened his horizons, contributing to the international characteristics of his team.
      Teams that have maintained long-term cooperation with Xie Qingguo’s group include the University of Chicago, UC Berkeley, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, Argonne National Laboratory, Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory in the United States, Moscow State Atomic Energy University in Russia, and CERN (European Organization for Nuclear Research).
      After returning to China, Xie Qingguo continued to lead his team towards the peak of PET technology. They completed innovations at three levels: electronics, core detection devices, and imaging systems, resulting in the world's first fully digital PET in 2010.
      The performance of full-digital PET surpasses that of the best similar products on the market, capable of detecting tumors one order of magnitude smaller than traditional PET. The technology was appraised by an expert team led by academicians as having reached international leading levels through a technical evaluation organized by the Hubei Provincial Department of Science and Technology. It also made it to the list of 'Top Ten Scientific Advances in China' jointly selected by the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the Chinese Academy of Engineering, and won the first prize for technological invention in Hubei Province in 2013.
      Fully digital PET has significant advantages over traditional PET. Just like how digital cameras replaced film cameras, the technological innovation is revolutionary. It not only boasts superior performance but more importantly, its technology itself becomes more open, allowing PET to develop in tandem with rapidly advancing computer and consumer electronics technologies, just as digital cameras do. The improvement in performance and the openness of system design will also make fully digital PET have a broader application prospect. Before achieving digitization, film cameras were single-functional, fixed in shape, and the process of taking photos and developing them was very complex. After achieving digitization, various new products such as digital cameras, DSLRs, and digital backs emerged continuously, and devices like phones and computers could all become cameras, with their applications penetrating every aspect of people's production and life, driving the rapid development of digital image processing technology. Similarly, after achieving digitization, PET's applications will be greatly enriched, making localized detection, portable detection, intraoperative detection, bedside detection, etc., possible.
 
      Creating a card for Optics Valley
      Before World Cancer Day on February 4, 2014, the World Health Organization (WHO) released the 'World Cancer Report,' which mentioned that the global cancer burden is increasing. In 2012, there were approximately 14 million new cases globally. It is predicted that by 2035, the number of annual new cancer cases will rise to 22 million, while the number of annual cancer deaths will increase from 8.2 million to 13 million during the same period. Among them, China had 3.06 million new cancer cases and 2.2 million cancer deaths, accounting for 20% and 25% of the global figures respectively, ranking first in the world. Due to a lack of early detection and diagnosis, these situations will become more severe.
      So, can Xie Qingguo's team's successfully developed cancer early diagnostic tool, which is hailed as the 'Two Bombs and One Satellite' of medical equipment, demonstrate its prowess on this unseen battlefield?
      Currently, PET has very limited application promotion in China. There are less than 200 clinical PET instruments currently in use nationwide, all imported from abroad, with the market monopolized by foreign manufacturers led by GPS (US GE, Dutch Philips, German Siemens), making both instrument acquisition and maintenance costs extremely high. Currently, a full-body PET/CT examination in China ranges between 8,000 to 14,000 yuan.
      If Xie Qingguo's team can mass-produce the PET, it will not only reduce prices, allowing ordinary people to afford medical care, but also determine diseases at an early stage of diagnosis, contributing significantly to saving more cancer patients' lives.
      In September 2013, Mayor Tang Liangzhi and his delegation visited Xie Qingguo's team's digital PET laboratory for a briefing. They immediately proposed the layout of clinical PET industrialization in Wuhan and the establishment of a digital PET application demonstration center. In the current situation where the PET market is monopolized by international giants, digital PET must rely on its technological advantages to rise up. The key step lies in promoting applications. 'PET is urgently needed for national economic development as medical equipment and indispensable for cutting-edge research as scientific instruments. If we can establish a digital PET application demonstration center and fully leverage our current performance advantages, it will be highly likely to generate new knowledge discoveries in basic biomedical and clinical medical research fields,' Xie Qingguo's team members introduced. 'Even Nobel Prize-level scientific discoveries might also be possible. At that time, the numerous barriers we face today would be broken through, and Chinese PET would stand tall among world leaders.'
      According to Mayor Tang's instructions, all parties quickly began negotiations and signed memorandums of understanding. The digital PET application demonstration center is ultimately planned to be located in Optics Valley Biocity Medical Device Industrial Park, aiming to complete construction and put it into use by 2014. Xie Qingguo's team's digital PET instruments already possess world-leading technology. If they can spread applications from this fertile land of Wuhan, there will be a high likelihood of establishing a digital PET application demonstration center in the Central China region, making digital PET a 'card for Optics Valley' and even a 'card for Wuhan.'
 
      Cultivating industry leading talents
      The digital PET industrialization cause has received strong support from the state and government. This is an immense encouragement to Xie Qingguo's team. However, at the same time, they also feel the pressure on their shoulders.
      Xie Qingguo's team members introduced that currently, international competitors are leveraging their scale advantages to fully pursue them. If we cannot establish a foothold ahead of them, China will miss the opportunity for PET to break through monopolies with technology and catch up with international competitors in one step. Therefore, the industrialization and application promotion of domestically produced full digital PET face severe challenges, making its advancement work urgent.
      A team starting from scratch is competing against powerful international giants. The task that digital PET faces is grand and arduous. However, the digital PET team firmly believes that what decides victory in this war lies with people. 'If the international giants have a solid fortress, we will cultivate the most elite troops!' For this reason, under the slogan of 'passion, dreams, courage, responsibility,' aiming to train future scientists and entrepreneurs, the digital PET team has consistently focused on talent development for industry over ten years.
      Currently, the average age of team members is less than thirty. The core guiding teachers include Professor Xie Qingguo and another teacher, both in their 70s, leading a group of 80s and 90s students to work hard. These two young professors proposed a team development plan of 'scientific discovery - technological invention - industrialization,' adhering to the principle that scientific research should drive industry. In 2013, both teachers actively applied for the '3551 Optics Valley Talent Plan,' hoping that under local government support, the digital PET's industrialization process could make greater strides.
      Under their leadership, the digital PET team innovatively implemented a project leader responsibility system. Regardless of whether it is major scientific research projects or work related to industrialization, they would be broken down into small projects, with student leaders proactively applying and forming project teams to promote them. Under this training model, graduates can become chief engineers in enterprises upon entering the workforce, some even leading their own teams to start businesses using technology accumulated in the laboratory, achieving product sales of nearly ten million yuan within two years.
      Professor Xie Qingguo believes that such practical exercises cultivate talents who can accomplish tasks. They are future leaders in the PET industry at Optics Valley Biocity, and they are scientific luminaries who add color to Wuhan's card...
