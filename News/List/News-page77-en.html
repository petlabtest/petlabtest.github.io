First digital positron emission tomography imager launched

The digital positron emission tomography (PET) with independent intellectual property rights developed by the research team of Professor Xie Qingguo of Huazhong University of Science and Technology has a higher resolution than the current commercial PET, which can detect tumors earlier and more sensitively and diagnose cancer...
2012-12-12
Digital PET Laboratory

NEWS RELEASE (By Correspondent Zhou Qianjin, Special Reporter Tu Xiaochen) Professor Xie Qingguo's research team from Huazhong University of Science and Technology has developed a digital positron emission tomography (PET) imaging device with independent intellectual property rights. This digital PET boasts higher resolution compared to current commercial PET devices, enabling earlier and more sensitive detection of tumors for cancer diagnosis. It is expected to bring breakthroughs in the prevention and early treatment of diseases such as cancer. Recently, the related paper titled 'Module Detector Implementation of a Fully Digital PET Data Acquisition System' has been accepted by the Journal of Nuclear Science and Technology.

Professor Xie Qingguo said that using the newly developed digital PET, researchers have completed studies on 13 mice models with lung cancer, liver cancer, ovarian cancer, etc., 16 mice models with Alzheimer's disease, and 30 normal mouse models. These studies comprehensively validated the instrument’s performance, especially confirming significant breakthroughs in spatial resolution. The best spatial resolution of commercial PET is 4.1 millimeters, capable of detecting lesions approximately 69 cubic millimeters in size. In contrast, if this digital PET were used for human imaging, its spatial resolution could surpass 1.5 millimeters, allowing detection of lesions smaller than 4 cubic millimeters.

It is reported that medical imaging devices such as ultrasound, computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) have long achieved digitization at the data acquisition source. However, due to the extremely high frequency signals and short display time PET needs to measure, existing regular-time sampling methods have always struggled to capture sufficient information, making it difficult to accurately reconstruct the signal being measured. Since 2001, Professor Xie Qingguo has led a cross-disciplinary team of 13 fields including medicine, engineering, and science for eleven years, inventing a multi-voltage threshold sampling method that accurately obtained the measurement signals and achieved precise image reconstruction. This process covers theoretical discovery to digital PET, industrial production of key detectors, assembly of commercial machines, and animal imaging experiments.

Professor Zhang Yongxue, President of the Nuclear Medicine Branch of China Nuclear Society and Director of Huazhong University of Science and Technology's Union Hospital PET Center, stated that any improvement in resolution represents a revolutionary breakthrough in medicine, meaning extended life for patients; digital PET can elevate the performance of PET systems to a new level, allowing earlier and more accurate diagnosis of diseases.
