What kind of growth environment do young people need? | Questioning 2020 · Year-end feature

Author | Ren Fangyan 2020 is the year after the wave...
2021-01-25

Author | Ren Fangyan


2020 was a year filled with rising stars.


"Chang'e-5" launched, and the youngest female commander at Wenchang Space Center, Zhou Chengjie, a '95s girl, became a trending topic; in the fight against the novel coronavirus pneumonia epidemic, among the 42,000 medical personnel who rushed to Wuhan, over 12,000 were from Generation Y...


In the field of scientific research, Cao Yuan, referred to as 'tsunami-level rising star,' published two papers on graphene in Nature in May 2020;


In June, Huawei announced a new batch of recruits for its 'Genius Youth' program; within two years, three graduates from Huazhong University of Science and Technology (hereinafter referred to as Huazhong UST) have been selected—Zuo Pengfei, Yao Ting, and Zhang Ji;


In December, Lu Chaoyang et al. from the University of Science and Technology of China's Pan Jianwei team made it onto Science for their quantum computing prototype 'Jiu Zhang'...


Whenever such young talents appear in public view, their background and growth paths always become a focal point. When the spotlight fades away, an issue with universal relevance remains worth pondering:



What kind of growth environment do today's youth need?

Setting Coordinates for Cao Yuans


 Cao Yuan once studied at the University of Science and Technology of China (hereinafter referred to as USTC) in its Young Talent Program. The 'Yan Jici Physics Elite Class' he was admitted into is one of the school's distinctive features—there are 16 similar science elite classes in total.

In a place teeming with talent, there is peer pressure, but it may also foster more meaningful common experiences.

In May 2020, China Science Daily published an article titled 'How Cao Yuans Are Made: Decoding the Elite Class Program at USTC,' which recounted through several young people's growth stories the cultivation philosophy of USTC's elite classes: encouraging experimentation, emphasizing fundamentals, and remembering tradition.

The author Yang Fan, who once worked in the Publicity Department of USTC, told China Science Daily that one reason for focusing on 'Cao Yuans' was that there are many outstanding young people at the school.

However, at USTC, each person receives a tailored development plan.

"Students come to USTC and take an entrance exam the day after they register," Yang Fan noted. Undergraduates who first arrive at USTC must take this test.

This helps determine where each student stands in the coordinate system, providing a basis for formulating personalized cultivation plans, 'like prescribing medicine.'

After officially starting their studies, every undergraduate has the opportunity to change majors or even do so multiple times.

This has also become a characteristic of USTC—providing as many exploration opportunities as possible to help students identify their interests early on.

The results are quite evident. According to statistics, half of the top 10% graduates from the School of Mathematical Sciences at USTC have changed majors; among the top 5% students in the College of Physics, 40% were originally non-physics majors; nearly a quarter of those who won the highest honor 'Guo Moruo Scholarship' are students who reselected their major...

Accumulation Regardless of Hot or Cold


 Coincidentally, there is another similar story in another news event.

In August 2020, ScienceNet WeChat public account published an article titled 'Three PhDs from Huazhong UST's Lab Selected for Huawei’s Genius Youth Program! Their Secret to Success Is...'.

Among them, Zhang Ji, who earned a doctorate from Huazhong University of Science and Technology after studying at a third-tier undergraduate institution, attracted much attention due to his 'perfect turnaround'.

"The quality of the seed should not be a decisive factor," recalled Xia Song, Secretary of the Party Committee and Director of the Wuhan National Laboratory for Optoelectronics (hereinafter referred to as Optical Center), when reviewing this report. Finding one's own development path is crucial, while mentors need to discover students' characteristics and strengths.

Multiple graduates from Huazhong UST have secured high-paying positions in succession, becoming a hot topic of discussion.

In Xia Song’s view, the 'coldness' or 'heat' of scientific research is relative.

At Huazhong UST, it was precisely because several generations had cultivated in the field of computer storage that the Information Storage Systems Ministry Key Laboratory where these 'rising stars' are located could maintain a unique competitive edge.

In Xia Song’s view, the reason why Optical Center can remain forward-looking is closely related to Huazhong UST's sense of mission.

"Even hot fields had their cold times. The key lies in whether there was good accumulation before they became 'hot,' which requires foresight and perseverance."

Xia Song also pointed out that there are many stories of starting from a 'niche' and becoming popular through long-term dedication: among them, the global most precise brain connectome map drawn by Chinese Academy of Sciences academician Luo Qingming and others is one such example.Another example is Professor Xie Qingguo and his team's decade-long efforts to promote digital PET in medical applications, which are exemplary cases of perseverance towards major needs and cutting-edge disciplines.。

The A and B Sides of the Era


 In the interview, Yang Fan also mentioned that current young researchers cannot be separated from their era.

Compared with previous generations, younger scholars today have better hardware conditions and more opportunities.

In some fields, researchers have shifted from being followers to leaders in the first echelon.

For those who are already at the forefront, becoming a leader means facing challenges of surpassing themselves.

In an era that calls for urgent independent innovation in core technologies, achieving breakthroughs has become more critical than ever.

In September 2020, the Future Science Prize was announced in Beijing.

At 96 years old, Professor Wang Zhenyi from Ruijin Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, told The Chinese Science Daily that he felt both joy and concern: 'Why is this award given to older people?' He expressed that rewarding younger scholars could encourage them to continue striving 'on the path we have paved, realizing our ideals.'

When interviewed again later, Wang Zhenyi reiterated his view on the importance of a research atmosphere for young talent.

However, in today's context, many young talents are working primarily to publish papers and aim at high-level publications.



Wang Zhenyi was straightforward: 'This is not how one makes achievements.'


The criteria for evaluating young scholars should focus on their ability to innovate and delve deeply into research, as well as whether they have the perseverance to endure solitude.


Both the evaluation system and the research atmosphere are fundamental factors that support innovation.


Yang Fan used brewing wine as an analogy in the interview: fine wine requires not only a master, yeast, and grain but also other elements such as fermentation mud, microbial communities, and environment.


For University of Science and Technology of China (USTC), 'fermentation mud' represents its accumulated cultural heritage over decades.


Innovative breakthroughs require dedicated efforts from generation after generation of young scholars who are passionate about their work.


Respecting the laws of scientific research and creating a relaxed environment for young talents to freely explore can better stimulate innovation potential, enabling them to quickly transition from 'new forces' in technological innovation to 'main forces,' continuously tackling challenges with determination and progressiveness, writing youthful chapters in scientific and technological innovation.
